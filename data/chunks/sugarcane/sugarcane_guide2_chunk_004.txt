Sugarcane requires full sunlight for good growth and sugar accumulation and performs poorly in shaded conditions. In terms of soil, sugarcane grows best in deep, well drained, fertile loamy soils, though it is a relatively adaptable crop compared to some others and can be grown across a fairly wide range of soil types, from sandy loams to heavier clay soils, provided drainage is adequate, since sugarcane does not tolerate prolonged waterlogging any better than most other crops and root damage from standing water can significantly reduce yield. Ideal soil pH for sugarcane is roughly six to seven point five, close to neutral, though the crop shows some tolerance for mildly acidic or mildly alkaline soils outside this range. Because sugarcane occupies the same field for a long period, often well over a year for the plant crop alone and then additional years if ratooned, good initial soil preparation and fertility management matter a great deal, since there are fewer natural opportunities to correct soil problems partway through the cycle compared to a short-duration annual crop. Sugarcane varieties are generally selected based on several key criteria that matter enormously to farmers and to the sugar industry.
