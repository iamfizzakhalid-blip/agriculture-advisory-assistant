Finally, the crop enters the maturation and ripening phase, during which vertical growth slows down substantially and the plant redirects its energy toward converting and storing sucrose in the stalk rather than building new tissue, and this is the period farmers watch most closely to determine when sugar content, referred to in the industry as the crop's brix or sucrose percentage, has reached its peak and the cane is ready to harvest. Sugarcane needs a genuinely hot climate and a long frost-free growing season to perform well, generally thriving with average temperatures somewhere between about twenty and thirty five degrees Celsius during active growth, though the ideal temperature during the final ripening phase is often somewhat cooler, since a period of cooler nights and reduced rainfall near maturity actually helps encourage the plant to accumulate more sugar rather than continuing vegetative growth. Sugarcane is highly frost sensitive and even a light frost can damage or kill the exposed parts of the plant and reduce sugar quality in the stalk, which is why it is mostly restricted to tropical and subtropical regions or warmer temperate zones with a sufficiently long frost-free window.
