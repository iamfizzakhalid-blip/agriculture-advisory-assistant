LODGING IN SUGARCANE Lodging (stalks falling over or leaning, sometimes tangling with neighboring stools) is a common problem in sugarcane, particularly in tall, vigorous varieties under high-input conditions, and can complicate harvesting, reduce sunlight interception for the lower canopy, increase disease risk (through increased humidity and stalk damage), and in some cases reduce final cane yield and quality. Common causes include excessive nitrogen application promoting tall, less sturdy growth; heavy wind and rain, particularly during the grand growth phase when stalks are actively elongating; and inadequate potassium supply, which affects stalk strength. Management practices to reduce lodging include balanced fertilization (avoiding excessive nitrogen relative to potassium), timely earthing-up (mounding soil around the base of stools) during early growth to improve anchorage, and selection of varieties with better standing ability where lodging is a recurring local problem. DETRASHING Detrashing (the periodic removal of dried, senesced lower leaves from the growing cane) is a common field operation in sugarcane cultivation, generally carried out a few times during the growing season. It improves airflow and light penetration within the crop canopy, which can help reduce humidity-favored disease and pest pressure and support better stalk development; it also facilitates other field operations such as irrigation, weeding, and, where relevant, application of fertilizers or crop protection products, and produces trash residue that can be used as mulch or, in some systems, is left as ground cover between rows.
