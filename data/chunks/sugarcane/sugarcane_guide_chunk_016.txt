HARVESTING Sugarcane is harvested when the crop reaches peak sucrose content, which varies by variety, planting time, and local climate but is generally assessed through a combination of crop age, external maturity indicators, and, in commercial systems, actual juice sucrose testing to time harvest for maximum sugar recovery. Harvesting significantly before optimal maturity results in lower sugar content and reduced value, while harvesting significantly after peak maturity can also lead to some decline in quality, particularly if the crop lodges, is damaged by frost or disease, or if the field is left standing too long after reaching peak ripeness. Harvesting can be done manually (cutting stalks close to ground level with a cane knife) or mechanically (using a mechanical harvester, which can cut, and in combine-type harvesters also chop and clean the cane in one pass). After cutting, cane deteriorates relatively quickly in terms of sucrose content and juice quality if not processed promptly, so minimizing the time between harvest and delivery to the mill for crushing is important to preserve sugar recovery. In regions where pre-harvest burning is practiced (to remove dry leaves and ease manual cutting, and sometimes to reduce pest/snake hazards), this must be timed very close to harvest and immediate transport to the mill, since burnt cane deteriorates especially quickly.
