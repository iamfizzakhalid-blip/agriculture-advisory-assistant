IRRIGATION REQUIREMENTS AND SCHEDULING Sugarcane has a high total water requirement due to its long growing season and large biomass, but water needs vary considerably across its growth stages: 1. Establishment (germination/sprouting) stage: Adequate, consistent soil moisture is needed to ensure good and uniform bud sprouting from the planted setts; both drought stress and waterlogging are damaging at this stage. 2. Tillering stage: Regular irrigation supports the development of an adequate tiller population, which is a key determinant of eventual stalk population and yield. 3. Grand growth (stalk elongation) stage: This is generally the period of peak water demand, since it corresponds to the most rapid vegetative growth and biomass accumulation; water stress during this phase can significantly reduce cane yield through reduced stalk elongation and thickness. 4. Maturation/ripening stage: Irrigation is often deliberately reduced or withheld toward the end of the growing season (where climate and soil moisture reserves allow), since a degree of moisture stress at this stage helps trigger the shift from vegetative growth to sucrose accumulation, improving sugar recovery; however, severe or prolonged drought stress at this stage can also reduce cane weight and juice quality if taken too far. Sugarcane is sensitive to waterlogging throughout its growth cycle, since standing water reduces root zone oxygen, damages roots, and can significantly reduce yield and increase disease susceptibility (particularly root and stalk rots); good field drainage is therefore as important as adequate water supply.
