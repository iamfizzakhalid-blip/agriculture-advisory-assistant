CLIMATE AND SOIL REQUIREMENTS Sugarcane is a tropical and subtropical crop requiring a long, warm growing season with abundant sunshine and adequate moisture for vigorous vegetative growth, followed by a period of relatively cooler, drier weather toward maturity to promote sucrose accumulation ("ripening") in the stalks. Optimum temperature for growth is generally in the range of about 20-35°C, with growth slowing considerably below about 20°C and being severely restricted or damaged by frost, to which sugarcane is highly sensitive, particularly in subtropical growing regions where occasional frost risk exists at the margins of the growing season. Sugarcane has a high water requirement due to its long growing season and large biomass production, generally needing substantially more total water over its crop cycle than most annual field crops, though actual requirement varies greatly with climate, soil, and irrigation efficiency. Sugarcane grows best on deep, well-drained, fertile soils with good water-holding capacity, such as medium to heavy loams; it can also be grown on a range of other soil types with appropriate management, but shallow, poorly drained, or heavily waterlogged soils are unsuitable, since sugarcane roots require adequate aeration and waterlogging causes root damage and poor growth. Soil pH in the range of roughly 6.0-7.5 is generally favorable, though sugarcane has moderate tolerance to somewhat acidic or alkaline conditions compared to some other crops. Deep soils are particularly valued because they support the extensive root system needed for a crop that remains in the ground for many months and, in ratoon systems, for multiple years. LAND PREPARATION Sugarcane land preparation is more intensive than for many annual crops, given the length of time the crop occupies the field and its establishment from vegetative setts rather than small seeds.
