Beyond refined sugar, sugarcane supports the production of jaggery/gur (a traditional unrefined sugar product important in some regional food cultures and economies), molasses (a by-product used in animal feed, fermentation industries, and ethanol production), and ethanol/biofuel, particularly significant in countries with established sugarcane-to-ethanol industries as a component of renewable fuel supply. Bagasse, the fibrous residue remaining after juice extraction at the mill, is commonly used as a fuel source for mill boilers (often making sugar mills substantially energy self-sufficient) and as a raw material for paper and other industrial products. In many sugarcane-growing regions, the crop is an important source of rural income and employment across cultivation, harvesting, and processing, and sugar milling is often a significant regional industrial and economic anchor.
