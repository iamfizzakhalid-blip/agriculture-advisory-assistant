The spores are spherical, echinulate, light brown in colour, measuring 6.5 to 8.5 microns in diameter. The smut spores are intermingled with groups of colourless, thin-walled sterile cells. The spores are disseminated by wind and they readily germinate in water or moist earth, producing a two- or three-celled promycelium from which terminal and lateral sporidia arise. The sporidia are narrow, elongated, straight, single-celled bodies joined to the promycelium by a short stalk. The disease is seed- as well as soil-borne. It can be transmitted through setts cut from diseased plants containing mycelium, as well as through spores that are in the soil. The mycelium of the fungus survives in the ratooned stumps of diseased canes, and fresh shoots which arise from such stumps are invariably smutted. Rogueing: the smutted whips should be collected carefully in sterilized bags, and burnt outside the field. The entire infected clump should be dug out and burnt. Avoid ratooning: the ratooning of infected crop should always be discouraged. Avoid sowing of susceptible varieties: the susceptible varieties of cane to this disease should not be grown. Co. 300, Co. 301, Co. 312, Co. 313, and Co. 513 have been proved susceptible for this disease; Co. 213, Co. 299, and Co. 290 are also susceptible varieties. Treatment of setts before sowing: prior to sowing, the setts should be dipped for five minutes in Bordeaux mixture 2:2:50, so that the smut spores adhering to them may be destroyed.
