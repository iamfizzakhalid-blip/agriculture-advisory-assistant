Nitrogen deficiency in sugarcane shows up as an overall pale yellowish green color, particularly in older leaves, along with reduced tillering and stunted growth, similar in principle to nitrogen deficiency symptoms in other cereals. Phosphorus supports strong early root and tiller development and is typically applied as a basal dose at planting time, since it does not move readily through the soil profile. Potassium is especially important in sugarcane because it directly supports sugar transport and accumulation within the stalk in addition to its usual roles in stalk strength and disease resistance, so potassium deficiency can specifically hurt sugar content even when cane tonnage looks reasonably good; deficiency symptoms typically appear as yellowing and scorching along the margins of older leaves. Sugarcane also has a relatively high demand for micronutrients over its long cycle, with iron, zinc, and manganese deficiencies occurring in some soils, often showing as pale interveinal yellowing or streaking on younger leaves, and these are generally corrected with targeted micronutrient fertilizer applications, either to the soil or as foliar sprays, once identified. Irrigation and water management in sugarcane must account for the crop's long duration and high total water need.
