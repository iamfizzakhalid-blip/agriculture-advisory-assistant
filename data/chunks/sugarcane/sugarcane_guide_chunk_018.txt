In the early establishment phase of a new sugarcane plant crop, before the canopy closes, intercropping with a short-duration crop (such as certain legumes or vegetables) is practiced in some systems to obtain additional income from the same land during the sugarcane's slower early growth period, provided the intercrop does not excessively compete with the establishing cane crop for light, water, and nutrients. MECHANIZATION IN SUGARCANE PRODUCTION Sugarcane production can involve mechanization at several stages, varying considerably by region, farm scale, and labor availability: tractor-drawn implements for land preparation, furrow opening, and earthing-up; mechanical planters in some large-scale operations, though manual sett placement in furrows remains common in many regions; sprayers for herbicide and crop protection product application; and mechanical harvesters in some large-scale, generally flatter and more consolidated production systems, which can cut, chop, and partially clean cane in a single pass, though manual harvesting with cane knives remains dominant in many smallholder and labor-abundant sugarcane-growing regions due to its selectivity, lower capital cost, and suitability to smaller or less uniform field conditions. ECONOMIC AND INDUSTRIAL IMPORTANCE Sugarcane is a major industrial and food crop globally, serving as the primary raw material for sugar production in most tropical and subtropical sugar-producing regions (with sugar beet being the other major global sugar source, mainly in temperate regions).
