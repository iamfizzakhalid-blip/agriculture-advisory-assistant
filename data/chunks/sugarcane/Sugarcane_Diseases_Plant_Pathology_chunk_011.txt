It is believed that sugarcane mosaic virus originated in the East and has been distributed from there in sugarcane cuttings to all the sugarcane growing countries of the world. At first pale patches or blotches appear in the green tissues of the leaves. These blotches are uniformly green throughout, but at the same time several shades of lighter colour surround them. They vary in shape and size; usually they are oval or oblong in outline, and their longitudinal axes remain parallel to the mid-rib. The characteristic symptoms are more distinct on the young unrolling leaves; thus symptoms are more distinct on newly opened leaves. In certain other cane varieties, mottling of the leaves appears; however, this mottling is not shown by all varieties. The entire plants become stunted and chlorotic, and may easily be identified from a distance. In India, the losses caused by this disease are 10 to 20 per cent. The sugarcane mosaic is caused by Sugarcane Mosaic Virus Brandes (1919). The virus is transmissible by mechanical means. Several vectors of aphid species have been reported; these vectors are Rhopalosiphum maidis Fitch and Aphis gossypii, among others. Sugarcane mosaic virus can be partially purified. Purified virus particles are rods measuring 650 to 770 by 12 to 15 millimicrons. The disease is seed-borne. It infects readily by sowing infected seed setts. Once the virus enters the host plant, it becomes systemic. Resistant varieties should be grown. Systematic rogueing of the infected canes should be done. Seed setts should be selected from a healthy crop. The wild hosts, such as Johnson grass (Sorghum halepense), should be eliminated. Quarantine regulations should be strictly followed.
