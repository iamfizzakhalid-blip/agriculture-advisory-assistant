Hot-water treatment: prior to sowing, the setts should be dipped in water at 50 to 52°C for 20 to 25 minutes, so that the setts become disease free. Healthy setts: the setts for sowing should be taken only from the healthy crops. Resistant varieties: the resistant varieties should be sown. Some resistant varieties are Co. 356, Co. 395, Co. 421, Co. 453, P. O. J. 7878, Cos. 76, and Cos. 109. Grassy Shoot Disease of Sugarcane The causal agent is Sugar Cane Grassy Shoot Virus. This disease was first observed in Maharashtra State (Vasudeva, R. S., 1956). Now it is a common disease and one of the serious problems of Maharashtra. The disease has also been recorded from Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Bihar, Orissa, Uttar Pradesh, and the Punjab. It has also been recorded from other countries such as Thailand and Taiwan. The characteristic symptom of the disease is the grassy appearance of the affected stools. Numerous thin, small grassy shoots give the plant a bushy appearance. Growth is arrested and the plants remain stunted. When most of the tillers are affected, the entire stool dries up. The grassy shoot disease of sugarcane is caused by Sugar Cane Grassy Shoot Virus. This virus is mechanically transmissible and aphid-transmitted. The aphid responsible for transmission is Aphis sacchari. The disease is transmitted from sugarcane to sorghum and from sorghum to sugarcane by this aphid. Seed selection: the seed setts should be selected from healthy canes. Sanitation: the diseased plants should be dug out and burnt. Spraying: insecticides should be sprayed to kill the aphids responsible for transmission of the virus.
