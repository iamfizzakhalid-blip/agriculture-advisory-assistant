Sugarcane pyrilla (leafhopper) - a sap-sucking pest that can build up rapidly under favorable conditions, causing yellowing and drying of leaves and honeydew excretion leading to sooty mold development, which further reduces photosynthesis; heavy infestations can noticeably reduce cane yield and juice quality. 3. Whitefly - sap-sucking insects that can build up on the underside of leaves, weakening the plant and promoting sooty mold through honeydew excretion, similar to their impact on other crops. 4. Mealybugs and scale insects - sap-sucking pests that can infest stalks and leaf sheaths, weakening the plant and, in some cases, being associated with the spread of certain viral diseases. 5. White grubs (root grubs) - the larval stage of certain beetles, feeding on roots, particularly damaging in the establishment and early growth phases, causing wilting, stunted growth, and, in severe infestations, plant death. 6. Termites - can damage setts before sprouting and attack the base of growing stalks, particularly in lighter soils and under moisture-stressed conditions, sometimes leading to lodging or death of affected stools. 7. Rodents - can cause damage to standing cane by gnawing at the base of stalks, in addition to general crop losses common to many field crops from rodent activity. Management principles: regular field monitoring/scouting to detect pest buildup early; use of resistant or tolerant varieties where available; field sanitation, including destruction of infested stubble and crop residue that can harbor pests between seasons or between the plant crop and ratoon; conservation of natural enemies as part of an integrated pest management approach; and judicious, threshold-based application of recommended insecticides where pest populations exceed damaging levels, following locally recommended products and timing.
