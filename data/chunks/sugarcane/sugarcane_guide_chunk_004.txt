LAND PREPARATION Sugarcane land preparation is more intensive than for many annual crops, given the length of time the crop occupies the field and its establishment from vegetative setts rather than small seeds. Typical steps include: 1. Deep plowing to break any hardpan and allow good root penetration, since the crop will occupy the field for many months to years across ratoon cycles. 2. Repeated harrowing/cultivation to break clods and produce a fine, well-pulverized seedbed, important for good sett-to-soil contact and uniform bud sprouting. 3. Leveling the field carefully, critical for uniform irrigation distribution over the long growing season and to avoid waterlogging in low spots, to which sugarcane is sensitive. 4. Opening furrows (trenches) at the appropriate row spacing, into which setts are laid for planting; furrow depth and spacing affect ease of irrigation, weeding, earthing-up, and eventual harvest. 5. Incorporating farmyard manure, compost, or other organic matter before furrow opening or at planting to improve soil fertility and structure, particularly valuable given the long duration and high nutrient demand of the crop. 6. Ensuring proper field drainage arrangements are in place before planting, given sugarcane's sensitivity to waterlogging and the length of the cropping cycle during which excess rainfall may occur.
