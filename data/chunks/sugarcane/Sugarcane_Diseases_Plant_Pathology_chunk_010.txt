The leaves dry up, followed by the stems, which become light and hollow. The wilt of sugarcane is caused by Cephalosporium sacchari Butler. The mycelium remains confined throughout the reddened portion of the stem. The hyphae penetrate all the cells, bundles, and parenchyma. The conidia are formed on short lateral branches in the vessels. The conidia are hyaline, oval to elliptical, occasionally curved or with one side flattened, measuring 4 to 12 by 2 to 3 microns. The conidiophores are of a definite shape, slightly swollen in the middle, with a blunt end. The conidia can germinate within 24 hours, producing a single terminal germ tube. This disease is both soil- as well as seed-borne. The disease is transmitted from one place to another by means of infected setts. The infected setts make the primary source of inoculum. When diseased setts are planted, either the eyes do not develop or the shoots arising from the eyes become wilted. Such setts produce a poor root system. The saprophytic fungus can survive in the soil for 2 to 3 years. Alkaline soil favours the disease. Seeds should be selected from a healthy crop. Ratooning should be discouraged. The crop should not be grown in alkaline soils. Resistant varieties should be sown. Prior to sowing, setts should be dipped in 40 ppm of Boron or Manganese. Sugarcane Mosaic The causal agent is Sugarcane Virus Brandes. In India, this disease has been recorded from all the sugarcane growing regions.
