Each perithecium contains numerous slender, elongated, club-shaped asci, mingled with fine paraphyses. Each ascus contains eight ascospores, arranged in 2 or 3 rows. The ascospores are elongated, 3-septate, and measure 20 to 24 by 5 microns. Predisposing Factors Unfavourable conditions of soil and moisture favour the disease. The disease is seen on thick canes; thin canes are never damaged. This is a soil-borne disease. The perithecia survive in the soil along with plant debris. Sanitation is of some use. Wilt of Sugarcane The pathogen is Cephalosporium sacchari Butler, class Deuteromycetes, order Moniliales, family Moniliaceae. In India, this disease was first recorded by Butler and Khan in 1913. This disease is of common occurrence in Bihar and eastern districts of Uttar Pradesh. It is also known in other countries such as the West Indies, United States, Mexico, and African countries. This disease produces distinct reddening of the cane pith, and is sometimes confused with red rot disease. On splitting the cane, the pith is found to have a diffused purple or dirty-red colour, which is provided with streaks, but never with the transversely elongated white patches characteristic of red rot. The colour is also not as bright as in red rot. In this disease the pith of the cane dries up more rapidly than when attacked by red rot. The pith becomes hollow and contains fluffy, greyish mycelium. When the cane is half grown, stunted clumps or even patches are seen in the field. Gradually the withering takes place.
