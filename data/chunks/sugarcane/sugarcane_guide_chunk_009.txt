The exact number and frequency of irrigations depends heavily on local rainfall, soil type, and irrigation method (flood/furrow irrigation is traditional in many regions, while drip irrigation, where available, can significantly improve water use efficiency and allows combined fertigation). FERTILIZER AND NUTRIENT MANAGEMENT Sugarcane has a high nutrient demand due to its large biomass production and long growing season, requiring substantial and well-timed nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium, along with attention to secondary and micronutrients on many soils. - Nitrogen: Critical for vegetative growth, tillering, and stalk elongation, and generally the nutrient most strongly linked to cane yield; because of the long growing season, nitrogen is typically applied in split doses over several applications (rather than all at once) - commonly a basal dose at or soon after planting, with subsequent doses applied during the tillering and grand growth stages, timed to support strong vegetative development while avoiding excessive late-season application that can delay ripening and reduce sucrose content. - Phosphorus: Important for root development, tillering, and early establishment; being relatively immobile in soil, it is typically applied as a basal/full dose at planting, placed in the furrow near the setts. - Potassium: Plays a significant role in stalk strength, disease resistance, and, importantly, sucrose accumulation and juice quality during the ripening phase; typically applied as a basal dose at planting, with a portion sometimes applied later in the season in higher-demand situations to support the ripening phase. - Micronutrients (such as zinc, iron, and manganese) can be deficient on certain soils, particularly calcareous or high-pH soils, and are corrected through soil or foliar application based on soil test results or observed deficiency symptoms. - Farmyard manure,
