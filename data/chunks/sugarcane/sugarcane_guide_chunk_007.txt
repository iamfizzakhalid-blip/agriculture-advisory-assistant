CROP GROWTH STAGES Understanding sugarcane's growth phases helps with timing of irrigation, fertilizer, and pest/disease management across its long growing cycle: 1. Germination/sprouting: Buds on the planted setts sprout and send up the initial shoots; a critical establishment phase sensitive to soil moisture, temperature, and sett quality/health. 2. Tillering: The initial shoots produce additional tillers from the base, building up the number of stalks per stool (clump); this phase largely determines the eventual stalk population and is important for a good, well-established stand before the crop closes canopy. 3. Grand growth (stalk elongation): The period of most rapid vegetative growth, with stalks elongating rapidly and the crop building the bulk of its biomass; this is generally the period of highest water and nutrient demand. 4. Maturation/ripening: Growth rate slows and the crop shifts toward sucrose accumulation within the stalk; relatively cooler temperatures, reduced nitrogen availability, and often reduced/withheld irrigation toward the end of this phase promote sugar accumulation ("ripening") rather than continued vegetative growth, which is important for achieving good sugar recovery at harvest. 5. Harvest maturity: The crop reaches peak sucrose content (varying by variety and local conditions) and is ready for harvest; harvesting significantly later than the optimum maturity window can result in some sucrose loss or deterioration, particularly if the crop lodges, is damaged by frost, or is affected by disease late in the season.
