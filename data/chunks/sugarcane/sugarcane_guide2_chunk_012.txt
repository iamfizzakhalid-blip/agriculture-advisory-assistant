Red rot spreads readily through infected setts used as planting material, which is one of the major reasons hot water or fungicide treatment of setts before planting, along with sourcing seed cane from disease-free nurseries, is so strongly recommended. Smut is another major disease, easily recognized by a long, black, whip-like structure that emerges from the growing point of an infected stalk in place of a normal shoot, and severely reduces both yield and the suitability of affected stalks for use as future seed cane. Ratoon stunting disease is a bacterial disease that does not always show obvious external symptoms but can cause a significant, sometimes hard-to-diagnose reduction in cane yield and vigor, particularly in ratoon crops, and is also spread mainly through infected setts and contaminated cutting tools, making sett treatment and tool sanitation important preventive measures. Various leaf diseases, including several fungal leaf spots and rusts, can also reduce photosynthetic leaf area, though they are generally less destructive than red rot, smut, or ratoon stunting disease.
