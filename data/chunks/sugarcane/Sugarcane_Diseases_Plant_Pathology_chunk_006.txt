Spegazzini, of class Ascomycetes, order Sphaeriales, family Mycosphaerellaceae. The mycelium of the fungus is inter-cellular and chiefly formed in the parenchymatous cells of the pith. The hyphae are thin, hyaline, septate, profusely branched, and contain oil globules. The acervuli, developed on the rind as well as on the mid-rib of leaves, are minute dot-like, black, and velvety. The long, septate, rigid, bristle-like setae arise from the hymenium of the acervulus; they are up to 200 microns long and 4 microns broad. Numerous clavate, single-celled hyaline conidiophores also arise from this hymenial layer; each conidiophore is 20 microns long and 8 microns broad. The conidia, which develop singly on the conidiophores, are single-celled, hyaline, falcate, thin-walled, granular, and measure 16 to 48 by 4 to 8 microns. In artificial cultures, terminal or intercalary, thick-walled, greenish-black chlamydospores are formed. On germination, the conidia produce single germ tubes at the tips of which the ovoid appressoria are formed. Water-logging conditions always favour disease incidence. Sometimes rains and humidity also favour the disease. The disease is seed-borne as well as soil-borne. It has been observed that the setts harbour the fungus and thus the disease is perpetuated from year to year. It has also been observed that the fungus is capable of growing and producing acervuli in soil, but the primary infection is thought to be mainly from infected setts. Secondary spread of disease takes place by means of conidia from the acervuli found on the mid-rib lesions. When the conidia settle on the leaves they germinate and invade the leaves through bruises and wounds. Stem infection takes place through insect borers and root-primordia.
