Soft, green leaves are favored by animals as fodder. The top leaves remain active, while dry leaves are known as trash or khori. Inflorescence The sugarcane inflorescence is arrow-shaped and takes the form of a spike, resembling Sarkanda. It develops under conditions of high temperature and humidity, and is notably found in areas such as Murree and Thattah (Sajawal). Primary Tillage Primary tillage for sugarcane involves 5–6 ploughings to break the hard pan, followed by 2–3 plankings and land levelling. Seed Bed Preparation In the wet method of seed bed preparation, a pre-soaking irrigation is applied to achieve optimum moisture, followed by 2 ploughings and planking. In the dry method, which follows fallow land, the field is dried and irrigated, followed by 1–2 ploughings and planking. Sowing Time Spring sowing takes place in February and March, with the crop maturing in February. Autumn sowing takes place in August and September, with the crop maturing in November or December. Seed Rate The recommended seed rate for sugarcane is 75,000 to 100,000 setts per hectare, equivalent to about 200–250 maunds per hectare, or 30–40 marlas per hectare for a normal crop. Sowing Methods Sugarcane can be sown using flat sowing, ridge sowing, or pit planting. Row arrangements include single row (60 cm spacing), double row (90 cm and 30 cm spacing), and triple row (120 cm and 30 cm spacing) patterns. Fertilizer The recommended fertilizer dose for sugarcane is 200:100-150:100 kg per hectare of NPK. One-third of the nitrogen is applied along with the full amount of phosphorus and potassium at sowing. The remaining nitrogen is split, with one-third applied at tillering, about two months after sowing, and the final one-third applied at earthing up, before the start of the rainy season.
