The crop thrives in hot, humid conditions typical of tropical and subtropical areas, requires ample sunlight, and goes through a dormant period during winter. Soil Requirements Sugarcane grows best in well-drained soils ranging from heavy to loam, with high organic matter content. Sandy soils can be made suitable by adding organic matter. The crop is sensitive to saline soils. Root Development Sugarcane has three types of roots. Primary or sett roots are shorter in length and shorter-lived, and are responsible for initial growth; they originate from the sett bands and are also called first roots. Secondary, tiller, or stem roots are longer in length and longer-lived, providing original growth; these develop after tillers form and provide nutrition and anchorage to the plant. Adventitious or fibrous roots can extend up to 2 metres. Shoot and Cane Development The sugarcane stem is jointed, with pithy internodes that may be thick, medium, or thin. Internodes are smaller at the base and top of the cane, while the internodes in the mid part are larger, though this can be affected by water stress or high temperature. Cane color can be white, pinkish, or greenish. Internode shapes vary and include cylindrical, tumescent, bobbin, conoidal or abconoidal, and curved forms. Leaf Sugarcane leaf blades vary in size and shape, and may be parallel, semi-erect, or drooping. Thick leaves tend to be erect or semi-erect, while thin leaves tend to be drooping or horizontal. Soft, green leaves are favored by animals as fodder. The top leaves remain active, while dry leaves are known as trash or khori.
