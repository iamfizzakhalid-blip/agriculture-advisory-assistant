Excessive irrigation right up to harvest, by contrast, tends to keep the crop growing vegetatively and can reduce the sugar percentage even if total cane tonnage looks high. As with other crops, waterlogging is harmful, and sugarcane fields need adequate drainage to prevent standing water from damaging the root system, particularly during the earlier stages of the crop cycle. Weed control is especially important early in the sugarcane cycle, roughly during the germination and tillering phases, before the crop's canopy has closed enough to shade out competing weeds on its own; this critical weed-free period can extend longer in sugarcane than in shorter duration crops like maize, sometimes lasting several months given how slowly the young cane crop initially develops compared to its eventual size. Weed management methods include pre-emergence and post-emergence herbicides suited to the local weed spectrum, inter-row cultivation, and mulching with crop residue or dried leaves stripped from the cane itself, sometimes called trash mulching, which both suppresses weeds and helps conserve soil moisture.
