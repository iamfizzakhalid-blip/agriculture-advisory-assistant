Inspection: the crop should be thoroughly inspected several times, and the infected plants should be destroyed by burning them. All the plants of a single stump should be destroyed. Avoid water-logging: the water-logging of the field should always be avoided, so that inoculum brought through the water may not infect the healthy plants. Inspection of setts: prior to sowing, the setts should be duly inspected. If any type of reddening is found, the sett should be discarded for sowing. Sanitation: sanitation practices should be adopted. After harvesting the crop, the stubbles and plant debris should be carefully destroyed by burning them. Crop-rotation: crop rotation of two years should be practiced, so that the inoculum present in the soil may be destroyed completely. Discourage ratooning: the ratooning of infected plants should always be discouraged. Avoid sowing of susceptible varieties: Co. 312, Co. 331, Co. 213, Co. 290, Co. 210, Co. 313, and Co. 513 have been proved susceptible for the disease. Whenever such varieties were sown, they succumbed; in the U. P. epidemic of 1939 to 1942, the Co. 210 and Co. 213 succumbed. Resistant varieties: only resistant varieties should be grown. Co. 453, Co. 393, Co. 356, and P. O. J. 7878 were found to be fairly resistant. Ring Spot of Sugarcane The pathogen is Leptosphaeria sacchari Breda de Haan, class Ascomycetes, order Pseudosphaeriales, family Pseudosphaeriaceae. This is a common disease of sugarcane in several parts of India, especially Uttar Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, West Bengal, Assam, and Maharashtra. This disease was first described in Java, and occurs in most other sugarcane growing countries such as the Philippines, the West Indies, and Australia. The disease symptoms appear soon after the rains
