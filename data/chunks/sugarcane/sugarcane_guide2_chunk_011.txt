The sugarcane top borer and internode borer cause similar tunneling damage at different parts of the plant. White grubs, the larval stage of certain beetles, feed on roots underground and can cause significant stand loss, particularly in sandy soils. Sugarcane woolly aphid is a sap-sucking pest that can build up heavily on the underside of leaves, weakening the plant and reducing sugar content, while also producing a sticky honeydew secretion that promotes sooty mold growth on the leaves. Termites can also damage both planted setts before germination and the base of growing stalks. As with other crops, regular field monitoring, resistant varieties where available, conservation of natural predators, and targeted rather than routine pesticide application are generally recommended over blanket spraying. Diseases are a serious and sometimes crop-threatening concern in sugarcane, made more significant by the fact that the crop is vegetatively propagated, meaning diseases present in planting material, the setts themselves, can be carried directly into a new crop rather than needing to be reintroduced fresh each season the way many seed-borne diseases work in annual crops. Red rot, caused by a fungus, is one of the most economically devastating sugarcane diseases in many regions, causing a reddish discoloration with characteristic white cross-bands visible when an infected stalk is split open lengthwise, along with a sour smell, wilting, and drying of leaves on affected stalks; this is a very important disease for a farmer
