Leaf spot and rust diseases - various fungal pathogens causing spotting or pustules on leaves, generally of lesser economic importance than red rot and smut but capable of reducing photosynthetic leaf area under severe infection. Management principles across sugarcane diseases: use of resistant or tolerant varieties as the primary defense, particularly important given sugarcane's long field duration and vegetative propagation, which can otherwise allow diseases to build up and spread efficiently across seasons; strict use of healthy, disease-free setts sourced from certified or well-managed seed nurseries, since sett-borne transmission is a major pathway for several key sugarcane diseases; hot water and/or fungicide treatment of setts before planting; prompt roguing (removal and destruction) of visibly diseased stools/plants when detected in the field to reduce further spread; crop rotation with non-host crops where feasible, particularly for soil-borne diseases; and avoidance of excessive nitrogen or waterlogged conditions that can predispose the crop to certain fungal diseases. MAJOR SUGARCANE PESTS 1. Sugarcane borers (a complex of stem borers, top borers, and root borers, varying by region) - among the most economically damaging sugarcane pests, with larvae boring into stalks, growing points, or roots, causing dead hearts (death of the central growing shoot) in young plants, hollowed or tunneled stalks that reduce cane weight and sugar content, and increased vulnerability to secondary rot infections entering through the bore holes; management relies on integrated approaches including resistant varieties, timely planting, field sanitation (destruction of infested stubble/residue that can harbor overwintering larvae), conservation of natural enemies, and targeted insecticide application based on monitoring and economic thresholds. 2.
