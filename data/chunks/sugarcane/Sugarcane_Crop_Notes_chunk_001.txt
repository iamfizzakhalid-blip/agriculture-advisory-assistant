Sugarcane — Crop Production Notes Introduction and Local Names The scientific name of sugarcane is Saccharum officinarum L., and it belongs to the family Poaceae. In Urdu, sugarcane is called Naishkar, and in Punjabi it is known as Ganna or Kamad. Species of Sugarcane Saccharum officinarum is known as American Cane, Saccharum sinense as Chinese Cane, Saccharum barberi as Indian Cane, Saccharum spontaneum as Wild Asian Cane, and Saccharum robustum as the Wild Cane of New Guinea. Crop Characteristics Sugarcane is a perennial, C4 crop with a determinate growth habit. It is cross-pollinated, and it is propagated using fuzz (true seed) or setts (stem cuttings). Composition Sugarcane is composed of 11–16% fibre, 12–16% soluble sugars, 2–3% non-sugars, and 63–73% water. Top Sugarcane Producing Countries According to FAO (2019) data, Brazil led sugarcane production with 37,300,000 tonnes, followed by India with 26,605,000 tonnes, China with 11,474,000 tonnes, Thailand with 10,024,000 tonnes, and the USA with 7,666,000 tonnes. Sugarcane Production in Pakistan (2019) In 2019, sugarcane was cultivated on an area of 1,270,000 hectares in Pakistan, with a total production of 83,000,000 tonnes and a yield of 65.3 tonnes per hectare. Pakistan ranked 6th in world sugarcane production that year. Importance Sugarcane is primarily grown for sugar extraction. Its bagasse is used to make chip board, paper, and hard board. Its molasses are used for alcohol production, and its tops and trash serve as fodder for animals. Sugarcane is also processed locally into gur, shakar, and desi cheeni. Climate Requirements Temperatures above 20°C are suitable for sugarcane growth, while temperatures below 20°C stunt its growth. The crop thrives in hot, humid conditions typical of tropical and subtropical areas, requires ample sunlight, and goes through a dormant period during winter.
