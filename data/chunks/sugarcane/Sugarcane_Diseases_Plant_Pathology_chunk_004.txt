Red Rot of Sugarcane The pathogen is Colletotrichum falcatum Wint., class Deuteromycetes, order Melanconiales, family Melanconiaceae. The perfect stage is known as Physalospora tucumanensis Spegazzini, class Ascomycetes, order Sphaeriales, family Mycosphaerellaceae. This disease is undoubtedly the most serious and destructive in India. It is of common occurrence in all countries where sugarcane is grown on a large scale. This disease is found in all the sugarcane growing states of the country. It occurred in a destructive form in eastern Uttar Pradesh and northern Bihar between 1939 and 1942, and in these years, the very existence of the sugar industry was threatened. The first external symptom of the disease is that the upper leaves of a shoot begin to lose colour and droop slightly. Thereafter they wither at the tip and withering progresses down the margins, leaving the centre green. All the parts above the ground are infected by the fungus, but the symptoms are conspicuously seen on the stem and midribs of leaves. The infected stems are completely rotted within; the rind loses its naturally bright colour, becomes dull in appearance, and shrinks at the nodes. Black dot-like structures, which are the acervuli of the fungus, appear on the shrunken internodes.
