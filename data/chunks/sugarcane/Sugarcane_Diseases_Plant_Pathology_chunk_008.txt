This disease was first described in Java, and occurs in most other sugarcane growing countries such as the Philippines, the West Indies, and Australia. The disease symptoms appear soon after the rains have commenced, when the plants are two to three months old. The first symptom is the appearance of small, discoloured, generally purple spots seen on both surfaces of the leaf. As these spots grow and expand at the margins, the central portion becomes dry. The margin consists of a narrow, reddish purple or brownish band, outside which there is sometimes a yellow areola, merging into the green of the leaf. The centre of the spot is dry and straw-coloured, and sharply marked off by the surrounding coloured ring. Small dot-like structures representing fructifications of the parasite are seen in rows on the straw-coloured spots. The spots are scattered over the whole or part of the leaf-blade, and when fully grown may measure 15 mm by 5 mm. The spots increase in size by the coalescence of neighbouring spots. Ultimately the leaf dries up and withers prematurely. The ring spot of sugarcane is caused by Leptosphaeria sacchari Breda de Haan. The mycelium is found within the leaf tissues. The hyphae enter the host cells and kill them, making the contents collect in a brown mass in the centre. Perithecia are formed in large numbers, chiefly within the rings; they are arranged in rows as black dots, between the finer veins of the leaf, and remain buried in the leaf tissues, except at the tip. The perithecia are spherical and about 140 microns in diameter. Each perithecium contains numerous slender, elongated, club-shaped asci, mingled with fine paraphyses.
