Weed management practices include: - Thorough pre-planting land preparation to reduce the weed seed bank and remove perennial weed roots/rhizomes. - Repeated interculture (hoeing/cultivation between rows) during the early establishment and tillering phases, often combined with "earthing-up" (mounding soil around the base of the stools), which helps control weeds, supports root development, and helps prevent lodging later in the season. - Herbicide application, including pre-emergence herbicides applied soon after planting to control early-germinating weeds, and post-emergence herbicides where appropriate for the weed spectrum present and compatible with the crop stage. - Mulching with trash (dried leaf residue from a previous crop or from detrashing operations) between rows, which can help suppress weed growth, conserve soil moisture, and moderate soil temperature. - In ratoon crops, prompt removal or management of the previous crop's trash/stubble residue and timely early-season weeding is particularly important, since ratoon regrowth can initially be slower and more vulnerable to weed competition than a fresh plant crop. MAJOR SUGARCANE DISEASES 1. Red rot - caused by the fungus Colletotrichum falcatum, one of the most economically damaging sugarcane diseases in many regions; causes internal reddening of the stalk tissue with characteristic white patches/cross-bands visible when the cane is split open, along with a distinctive sour smell, wilting, and drying of leaves; spreads through infected setts (making sett selection from disease-free seed crops critical) and can also spread in the field under favorable warm, humid conditions; severely reduces both cane yield and sugar recovery in infected stalks. 2.
