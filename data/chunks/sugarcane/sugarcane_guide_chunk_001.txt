Sugarcane Cultivation Complete Guide: Botany, Varieties, Planting, Fertilizer, Irrigation, Diseases, Pests, Growth Stages, Harvesting, Ratooning, and Yield Improvement INTRODUCTION TO SUGARCANE Sugarcane (Saccharum officinarum and related Saccharum hybrids, most commercially grown cane today being complex interspecific hybrids) is a tall, perennial grass cultivated primarily for its stalks, which store high concentrations of sucrose used to produce sugar, and secondarily for jaggery/gur (unrefined sugar), ethanol/biofuel, and other by-products. It belongs to the family Poaceae, the same family as wheat, maize, and rice. Unlike most annual field crops, sugarcane is a semi-perennial crop: after the main (plant) crop is harvested, the stubble left in the ground can regrow into one or more subsequent "ratoon" crops without replanting, which is a distinctive and economically important feature of sugarcane cultivation. Sugarcane has a long growing cycle, typically taking around 10-12 months (and sometimes longer depending on variety and climate) to mature from planting to harvest. BOTANICAL CHARACTERISTICS Sugarcane is a tall grass that can grow to a height of several meters. It has a fibrous root system, with primary "sett roots" emerging from the planted stem cutting (sett) at establishment, later replaced by a more extensive system of "shoot roots" that develop from the base of each new tiller/shoot as the crop establishes and grows. The stalk (cane) is composed of a series of nodes and internodes; sucrose accumulates within the internodal tissue, particularly in the lower and middle portions of the stalk, and cane thickness, internode length, and juice content are key factors in yield and sugar recovery.
