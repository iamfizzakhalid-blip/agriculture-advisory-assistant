PLANTING TIME Sugarcane planting time varies considerably by region and is generally categorized into distinct planting seasons (such as spring and autumn planting in subtropical regions with a defined winter, or planting tied to the onset of favorable rains in more tropical regions). The choice of planting season affects the length of the growing period available before the crop is harvested and can influence yield and sugar content, with different regions and cropping systems following locally established recommended planting windows based on temperature and rainfall patterns; farmers should follow local agricultural extension guidance for the recommended planting time(s) in their specific area, since planting outside the recommended window can result in poor establishment (if too cold) or a shortened growing period (if delayed), both reducing final yield. PLANTING METHOD AND SETT RATE Sugarcane is planted by laying setts (stem cuttings with buds) end-to-end or overlapping in furrows and covering them with soil. - Furrow (row) spacing commonly ranges from about 60-120 cm depending on variety, region, mechanization level, and irrigation method, with wider spacing sometimes used to facilitate mechanical operations and intercropping in the early stages before the cane canopy closes. - Setts are typically planted with 2-3 buds/nodes per piece, laid in a continuous or slightly overlapping line within the furrow to ensure adequate bud density for good stand establishment. - Setts should be covered with an appropriate depth of soil (commonly a few centimeters) - too shallow risks desiccation of the sett and poor sprouting, while too deep can delay or reduce emergence. - Sett treatment before planting (hot water treatment and/or fungicide dip) is widely recommended to reduce sett-borne diseases, particularly smut and red rot, and can improve germination/sprouting percentage. - After planting, light irrigation is generally needed to ensure adequate moisture for bud sprouting, especially
