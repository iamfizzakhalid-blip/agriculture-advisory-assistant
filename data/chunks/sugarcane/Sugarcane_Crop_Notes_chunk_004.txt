The remaining nitrogen is split, with one-third applied at tillering, about two months after sowing, and the final one-third applied at earthing up, before the start of the rainy season. Irrigation Sugarcane sown in March requires 16–18 irrigations, while sugarcane sown in September requires 20–22 irrigations. The irrigation method used depends on water availability and rainfall. Weeds Weed management in sugarcane relies on cultural control, chemical control, and biological control, applied through pre-emergence and post-emergence methods. Insect Pests Borer attack can be severe in sugarcane. Management includes the use of systemic granular insecticides when the crop is 1–2 feet high, as well as biological control agents. Diseases Sugarcane is affected by diseases such as red rot and whip smut. These are managed through seed treatment and the use of resistant varieties. Harvesting Sugarcane sown in March is harvested in January, while sugarcane sown in September is harvested in October or November. Harvesting operations include stripping, trashing, and topping. Sugarcane Varieties by Province Punjab: Approved varieties include CP 77/400, CP 43/33, CPF 237, CPF 243, CPF 247, SPF 231, SPF 234, SPF 245, SPF 246, SPF 247, HSF 240, and COJ 284. Prohibited varieties in Punjab include CO 1148 (Indian), Triton, COL 29, COL 44, COL 54, BL 4, L 116, L 118, BF 162, and SPF 238. Sindh: Approved varieties include Thattha 10, Larkana 1, Larkana 2004, CPF 237, L 126, HS 2, HS 4, HS 12, and B 129 (Gulabi 95). Prohibited varieties in Sindh include COL 29, COL 54, NIA 98, and Disco 843/60. KPK: Approved varieties include CP 77/400, Mardan 92, Mardan 93, Bannu 1, SPSG 394, MSP 421, and CP 72-2086. Prohibited varieties in KPK include COS 245, COS 321, NOC 310, and NS 98.
