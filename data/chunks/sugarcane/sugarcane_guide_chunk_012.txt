Smut - caused by the fungus Sporisorium scitamineum (formerly Ustilago scitaminea); produces a characteristic long, black or grey, whip-like structure ("smut whip") emerging from the growing point of infected shoots, covered in black spore masses; infected plants show stunted, thin, grassy shoot proliferation and reduced yield; spreads through infected setts and windborne spores, and is managed primarily through resistant varieties, use of disease-free planting material, and roguing (removal and destruction) of infected stools when detected. 3. Ratoon stunting disease - a bacterial disease that causes stunted growth and reduced yield, particularly noticeable in ratoon crops (hence the name), often without obvious external symptoms, making it a somewhat insidious yield-reducing disease; spreads through infected setts and can be reduced through hot water treatment of setts and use of disease-free planting material. 4. Mosaic - a viral disease causing a characteristic light and dark green mottled/mosaic pattern on leaves, generally reducing vigor and yield to varying degrees depending on variety susceptibility and virus strain; spreads through infected setts and via aphid vectors, managed mainly through resistant varieties and disease-free planting material. 5. Pokkah boeng - caused by Fusarium species, affecting the growing point and young leaves, causing distorted, twisted, or chlorotic young leaves and, in severe cases, top rot; generally favored by high humidity and can be more damaging in wet growing seasons. 6. Wilt - caused by Fusarium species affecting the vascular system, causing yellowing, wilting, and drying of stalks, generally more severe under moisture stress or other predisposing stress conditions. 7. Leaf spot and rust diseases - various fungal pathogens causing spotting or pustules on leaves, generally of lesser economic importance than red rot and smut but capable of reducing photosynthetic leaf area under severe infection.
