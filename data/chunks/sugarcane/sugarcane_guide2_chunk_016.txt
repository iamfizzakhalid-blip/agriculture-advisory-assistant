Yield potential varies enormously with climate, variety, and management, with cane tonnage commonly ranging from as low as thirty to forty tons per hectare in less favorable rainfed conditions with older varieties, up to well over one hundred tons per hectare in well managed, irrigated systems using modern high-yielding varieties. Because sugarcane is almost always grown under contract with, or in close coordination with, a specific sugar mill that will process the harvested cane, farmers typically need to plan their planting and harvest timing around the mill's crushing season and capacity, and payment is often based not just on the tonnage of cane delivered but also on its measured sugar content, meaning that management practices that boost sucrose percentage, such as appropriate late-season water reduction and balanced potassium nutrition, directly affect a farmer's income in a way that is somewhat more direct than in most grain crops, where payment is typically based on weight and basic quality grading alone rather than a specific internal chemical content measurement.
