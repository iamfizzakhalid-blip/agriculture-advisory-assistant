In the infected capsules, the bacteria penetrate to the developing seed and form slimy masses around the hairs surrounding the micropyle. This is a seed-borne disease. The primary infection takes place through seed-borne bacteria. The bacteria form slimy masses around the hairs surrounding the micropyle of the seed. On germination of contaminated seed, the bacteria infect the cotyledons as they emerge through the soil and produce elongated marginal lesions. Thereafter, the leaves and stems become infected. The bacteria do not survive long in the soil. In plant debris the organism can survive for a few weeks, and sometimes serves as a means of inoculation. The spread of the disease depends much upon climatic conditions. In rainy weather the leaves and stems are attacked, especially when the rains are heavy and accompanied by strong winds. The pathogen is carried by wind-driven rain onto the plants. The most important factors favourable to the pathogen are high relative humidity and high air temperature. Since the disease is seed-borne, the seeds should be treated with organo-mercurials prior to sowing, as a seed treatment. The seeds may also be soaked overnight in 1000 ppm of streptomycin sulphate. Since the bacteria can survive for a few weeks in plant debris, sanitation should be practiced: the affected portions should be collected and destroyed. Regarding resistant varieties, these should be evolved; in India, work is in progress in this direction with good prospects.
