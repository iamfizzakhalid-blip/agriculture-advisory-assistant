Jassids/leafhoppers - sap-sucking insects that cause a characteristic yellowing/reddening and downward curling ("hopperburn") of leaf margins, reducing photosynthetic capacity and, in severe infestations, causing significant vegetative and yield loss. 4. Aphids - sap-sucking insects that cluster on new growth and the underside of leaves, weakening the plant, excreting honeydew that promotes sooty mold, and in some cases contributing to virus transmission. 5. Thrips - small insects that feed on young leaves and growing points, causing leaf distortion and silvering, particularly damaging to young seedlings. 6. Mealybugs - sap-sucking insects that can infest stems, leaves, and bolls, particularly in some regions where they have emerged as a significant pest, causing stunting, leaf curling, and honeydew-related sooty mold; often spread partly through ants that protect them in exchange for honeydew. 7. Mites (particularly spider mites) - very small pests that cause stippling, yellowing (bronzing), and eventual leaf drop under heavy infestation, generally favored by hot, dry conditions. Management principles: Integrated Pest Management (IPM) approaches combining regular field scouting to detect pest buildup before economic thresholds are reached; use of resistant/tolerant varieties, including Bt cotton for bollworm management where appropriate, along with mandated non-Bt refuge planting to slow resistance development; conserving natural enemies (predatory insects, parasitic wasps) that help suppress pest populations naturally; avoiding excessive nitrogen application, which promotes lush growth attractive to sucking pests; timely sowing and harvesting to reduce the overlap between vulnerable crop stages and peak pest activity; and judicious, threshold-based use of insecticides with attention to selecting products that minimize harm to natural enemies and pollinators, and rotating modes of action to slow the development of insecticide resistance in pest populations.
