Cotton is highly sensitive to waterlogging at any growth stage, since standing water reduces root zone oxygen and quickly leads to root damage, shedding of squares and bolls, and stunted growth; ensuring good field drainage and avoiding over-irrigation is as important as ensuring adequate water supply. The exact number and timing of irrigations depends heavily on soil type, rainfall pattern, and irrigation method (flood, furrow, sprinkler, or drip), with drip irrigation generally offering the most efficient water use and the ability to combine irrigation with fertilizer application (fertigation). FERTILIZER AND NUTRIENT MANAGEMENT Cotton requires a balanced supply of nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, and, on many soils, micronutrients such as zinc and boron, for healthy vegetative growth balanced with strong reproductive (square, flower, and boll) development. - Nitrogen: Supports vegetative growth, leaf area, and overall plant vigor; however, excessive nitrogen, particularly late in the season, promotes excessive vegetative ("rank") growth at the expense of square and boll retention, delays maturity, and increases susceptibility to certain pests and diseases due to a dense, humid canopy. Nitrogen is generally applied in split doses, with a portion at or soon after sowing and the remainder during the vegetative-to-squaring and flowering stages, timed to support reproductive development without excessive late-season vegetative growth. - Phosphorus: Supports root development, energy transfer, and early plant establishment; because phosphorus is relatively immobile in soil, it is generally applied as a basal dose at or before sowing. - Potassium: Plays an important role in boll development, fiber quality, and the plant's ability to withstand stress; adequate potassium supply supports proper boll filling and reduces the incidence of certain physiological disorders sometimes associated with potassium deficiency
