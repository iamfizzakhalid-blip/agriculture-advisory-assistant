Optimum temperature for germination is around 18-30°C, with growth being favored by warm days (around 25-35°C) and moderately warm nights; temperatures below about 15°C slow growth and germination considerably, and frost damages or kills the crop, making cotton highly sensitive to cold at both the start and end of the season. Cotton requires a relatively long frost-free period, generally around 180-200 days from sowing to final picking depending on variety and region. While cotton has moderate drought tolerance once its deep root system is established, adequate moisture is essential during flowering and boll development for good yield; conversely, excessive rainfall or high humidity during boll opening and picking promotes boll rot and lint quality deterioration, and waterlogging at any stage severely damages the crop. Cotton grows best on deep, well-drained soils with good water-holding capacity, such as medium to heavy loams and clay loams; black cotton soils (vertisols), rich in clay and known for their moisture retention, are traditionally associated with cotton cultivation in several regions. Soil pH between about 6.0 and 8.0 is generally suitable, and cotton has moderate tolerance to soil salinity compared to many other crops, though yield and quality decline under high salinity. Poorly drained or waterlogged soils are unsuitable, as cotton is highly sensitive to root zone waterlogging, which causes root damage, shedding of squares (flower buds) and bolls, and stunted growth.
