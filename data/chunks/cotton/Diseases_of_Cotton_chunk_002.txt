From these stromatic masses of hyphae, tufts of conidiophores are given out, usually through the stomata. The conidiophores are up to 150 microns long, flexuous, dark-brown, and septate. The conidia develop at the apices of conidiophores; they are hyaline, long, slender, curved bodies, narrow above and rounded below, 5 to 7 times septate, and measure 70 to 180 by 3 microns. The perfect perithecial stage is represented by an ascomycete, Mycosphaerella gossypina (Atk.) Earle, which has been found in the United States and China. The perithecia are ovate, black, partly sunken in the tissue, and measure 60 to 70 by 65 to 91 microns. The asci are sub-cylindric and measure 8 to 10 by 40 to 45 microns. The ascospores are elliptic with slight constrictions at the septa, measuring 15 to 18 by 3 to 4 microns. This is a soil-borne disease. The mycelium and conidia survive in the soil and infect the plants in the coming season. Wet soil and misty weather favour the disease incidence. Attack on the young parts of the plant usually indicates want of vigour or an unfavourable season. The exotic varieties of cotton are immune to the disease. Since the disease is soil-borne, sanitation should be practiced: all plant debris should be collected and destroyed by burning it. Root Rot of Cotton The pathogen causing root rot of cotton is Rhizoctonia bataticola (Taub.) Butl., also known by the names Rhizoctonia solani and Macrophomina phaseolina. In its systematic position, it belongs to Class Deuteromycetes, Order Sphaeropsidales, Family Sphaeropsidaceae. This disease occurs in several countries of the world, such as Venezuela, U. S.
