CROP GROWTH STAGES Understanding cotton's growth stages helps with timing of irrigation, fertilizer, and pest/disease management: 1. Germination and emergence: Seed absorbs moisture and the seedling emerges above the soil surface; a critical and vulnerable stage sensitive to soil crusting, cold soil, and seedling pests/diseases. 2. Seedling establishment: Development of the first true leaves and initial root system expansion. 3. Vegetative growth (squaring initiation): The plant develops main-stem leaves and begins forming vegetative and fruiting branches; the first flower buds (squares) appear on fruiting branches. 4. Squaring: Flower buds (squares) continue to develop and increase in number; this stage is particularly vulnerable to sucking pests and early bollworm attack, since damage to squares directly reduces potential boll (and yield) numbers. 5. Flowering: Squares open into flowers, which are pollinated (mostly self-pollination, some insect-assisted) and then wilt within about a day, with the base of the flower developing into a young boll. 6. Boll development: Fertilized ovaries develop into bolls that enlarge and mature over several weeks; this is a critical period for water and nutrient supply, as stress here directly reduces boll size, seed cotton weight, and fiber quality. 7. Boll maturation and opening (boll burst): Mature bolls dry down and split open, exposing the fluffy lint, which is the point at which picking begins for that boll; because flowering and boll set occur over an extended period on a single plant, picking is typically done in multiple rounds over several weeks to months as different bolls mature. 8. End of season/defoliation (where practiced): In some systems, particularly where machine picking is used, a defoliant may be applied
