Produces small, angular, water-soaked lesions on leaves that turn brown/black, can spread to stems and bolls causing boll rot and reduced fiber quality, and is favored by warm, humid, rainy conditions; seed-borne and residue-borne, so it is managed through resistant varieties, use of disease-free treated seed, and crop rotation/residue management. 3. Fusarium wilt - caused by Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. vasinfectum, a soil-borne fungus that invades the vascular system, causing yellowing, wilting, and often death of infected plants, particularly under conditions of nematode co-infection or plant stress; management relies mainly on resistant varieties and crop rotation, since the fungus can persist in soil for extended periods. 4. Verticillium wilt - caused by Verticillium dahliae, another soil-borne vascular wilt fungus causing leaf yellowing, wilting, and defoliation, generally favored by cooler soil temperatures than Fusarium wilt; managed through resistant varieties and crop rotation. 5. Seedling diseases (damping-off/seedling blight) - caused by various soil-borne fungi (such as Rhizoctonia, Pythium, and Fusarium species) affecting germinating seed and young seedlings, particularly under cool, wet soil conditions; managed through seed treatment with fungicide, avoiding sowing into excessively cold or wet soil, and ensuring good field drainage. 6. Boll rots - caused by various fungal and bacterial organisms that infect developing or maturing bolls, often entering through insect feeding wounds or in conditions of high humidity and rainfall during boll development and opening; management includes insect pest control (to reduce entry wounds), balanced nitrogen management (avoiding excessive vegetative growth that increases canopy humidity), and adequate plant spacing for airflow.
