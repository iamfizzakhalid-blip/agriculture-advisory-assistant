The disease is restricted to the black cotton soils, which are heavy clay with an alkaline reaction, but in the light alkaline and loamy soils it is unknown. The fungus attacks the host when the plants are 1 to 3 weeks old. The disease becomes manifest when the plants are 5 to 6 weeks old, at which time the leaves become yellow and wither, until finally the entire plant dries up. The plants soon wilt and die. In young affected plants the basal part becomes black, whereas in mature plants examined, partial discolouration is seen. If an attacked plant is pulled up and examined, it will be found that the vascular tissues of the roots and stem are blackened because of the plugging of vessels. As a rule the affected plants succumb completely to the disease, but cases of partial infection are also not uncommon. When examined, the transverse sections of the affected stem show that the vascular bundles are seen plugged with fungal hyphae and a gummy substance. A wilted plant shows blackened roots and wilted leaves. In transverse section, the mycelium can be seen within the vessels of the infected plant, along with the microconidia and macroconidia of the fungus. The wilt of cotton is caused by Fusarium oxysporum f. vasinfectum (Atk.) Snyder and Hansen. The fungus is found both inter- and intracellularly in the host tissue. The mycelium of the fungus plugs the xylem vessels. Hyphae run through the vessels and permeate the stem and the complete root system.
