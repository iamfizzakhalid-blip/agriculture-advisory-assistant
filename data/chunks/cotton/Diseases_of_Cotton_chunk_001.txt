Chapter 17: Diseases of Fibre Crops Cotton (Gossypium Linn.) Leaf Spot Disease of Cotton The pathogen causing this disease is Cercospora gossypina Cke. In its systematic position, it belongs to Class Deuteromycetes, Order Moniliales, Family Dematiaceae. The disease is found in many countries of the world such as the United States, West Indies, Egypt, China, and other cotton growing countries. In India, this disease is found sporadically in cotton growing states. The disease may appear at any stage in the growth of the plant, but chiefly when plants are fully grown. The rounded or irregular spots appear on both surfaces of the leaf, and are generally 4 mm in diameter. They are at first yellowish-brown, then whitish in the centre from the withering of the tissues. The margin of the spot consists of a distinct, dark brown or a blackish rim which in young spots may be reddish in the beginning. The adjacent spots may coalesce with each other, forming large patches of the leaf to wither. In old spots, the centre cracks or breaks, forming a hole. Badly infected leaves lose their green colour, turn yellow, and ultimately wither and drop off. An infected leaf shows numerous leaf spots on its surface, with black dots visible within the spots that represent tufts of conidiophores and conidia. The conidiophores are slender and septate, and the conidia are transversely septate and elongated. The leaf spot disease of cotton is caused by Cercospora gossypina Cke. The mycelium is strictly confined to the spots. The hyphae are intercellular, hyaline, and septate. After growth for a time, small stromatic masses of dark hyphae collect near the surface, chiefly on the upper side of the leaf.
