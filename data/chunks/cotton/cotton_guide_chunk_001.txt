Cotton Cultivation Complete Guide: Botany, Varieties, Sowing, Fertilizer, Irrigation, Diseases, Pests, Growth Stages, Picking, and Yield Improvement INTRODUCTION TO COTTON Cotton (Gossypium species, mainly Gossypium hirsutum - American upland cotton, and Gossypium arboreum/Gossypium herbaceum - desi/Asiatic cotton) is one of the world's most important fiber crops and also a significant oilseed crop, since cottonseed yields edible oil and protein-rich cake used as livestock feed. Cotton belongs to the family Malvaceae. It is grown mainly as a kharif (summer) crop in South Asia, sown in spring/early summer and harvested from autumn into winter, with picking often continuing over several months as bolls mature progressively. Cotton fiber (lint) is the raw material for the textile industry, while cottonseed is processed for oil and animal feed, and cotton stalks/residue can be used as fuel or organic matter after harvest. BOTANICAL CHARACTERISTICS Cotton is a perennial shrub grown as an annual crop in commercial cultivation. It has a deep, well-developed taproot system with lateral roots, allowing it to draw moisture and nutrients from a considerable soil depth, which contributes to its relative drought tolerance compared to many other crops once established. The stem is woody at the base and branches into vegetative (monopodial) branches lower on the plant and fruiting (sympodial) branches higher up, which bear the flowers and bolls. Cotton is largely self-pollinated, though some cross-pollination by insects (especially bees) occurs.
