Farmers should select varieties recommended by local agricultural research institutes for their specific region, since disease and pest pressures (particularly viral diseases spread by insect vectors) vary significantly by location and change over time as new pest biotypes or disease strains emerge. Certified seed from a reliable source is recommended over farm-saved seed, both for genetic purity and, in the case of Bt cotton, to ensure the seed actually carries the intended trait and appropriate refuge seed requirements (where mandated) are met to slow the development of pest resistance to the Bt trait. SOWING TIME Cotton is generally sown in spring to early summer once soil and air temperatures are reliably warm enough for good germination and there is no further risk of frost, with exact timing varying considerably by region and local climate. Sowing when soil temperature is too low results in poor, slow, and uneven germination and increases seedling vulnerability to soil-borne diseases and pests. Sowing too late shortens the effective growing season, delaying flowering and boll development into periods of less favorable temperature or approaching the end of the frost-free window, which can reduce yield and delay or compromise the harvest of later-formed bolls. Local agricultural extension recommendations should be followed for the optimal sowing window, as it is tightly linked to local temperature patterns and the risk of late spring frosts or early autumn frosts.
