Weed management practices include: - Thorough pre-sowing land preparation to reduce the existing weed seed bank and remove perennial weed roots/rhizomes. - Timely and repeated interculture (hoeing/cultivation between rows) during the early vegetative period, since the first 45-60 days after sowing are generally the most critical period for weed competition in cotton. - Herbicide application, including pre-emergence herbicides applied at or shortly after sowing to control early-germinating weeds, and post-emergence herbicides where appropriate and compatible with the crop stage and, where relevant, any herbicide-tolerance trait in the variety being grown. - Mulching (organic or plastic) in some systems to suppress weed growth and conserve soil moisture, particularly in smaller-scale or high-value production systems. - Maintaining clean field borders and irrigation channels to reduce weed seed spread into the field. Weeds compete with cotton for light, water, and nutrients, and can also serve as alternate hosts for certain cotton pests and diseases, making weed control relevant to overall pest and disease management as well as direct yield protection. MAJOR COTTON DISEASES 1. Cotton leaf curl virus (CLCuV): A viral disease transmitted by the whitefly (Bemisia tabaci), causing upward or downward curling of leaves, thickening and vein swelling (enations) on the underside of leaves, stunted growth, and significant yield loss where the disease and its vector are prevalent; management relies heavily on using resistant/tolerant varieties, controlling the whitefly vector population, and removing/destroying severely infected plants to reduce virus spread. 2. Bacterial blight (angular leaf spot) - caused by Xanthomonas citri pv. malvacearum (formerly Xanthomonas campestris pv. malvacearum).
