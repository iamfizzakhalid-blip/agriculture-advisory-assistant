The fungus attacks the roots of the host plant. As soon as the hyphae establish in the roots, they multiply rapidly in the cortical tissues, extend up to the pith, and ultimately enter the xylem vessels. The pathogen survives in the soil, and when the cotton crop is sown again in infested soil, the fungus infects the plants. Since the pathogen survives in the soil for many years, a suitable crop rotation is of much use for controlling the disease. The sowing of resistant varieties is the best method of control, but sufficient work has not been done in this direction. As a cultural practice, the sowing dates of the crop may be so adjusted that the seedling stage is avoided when the soil temperature is high, above 35 degrees Celsius. Mixed cropping with sorghum and Phaseolus is helpful in reducing the soil temperature. Wilt of Cotton The pathogen causing wilt of cotton is Fusarium oxysporum f. vasinfectum (Atk.) Snyder and Hansen, with the synonym F. vasinfectum Atk. In its systematic position, it belongs to Class Deuteromycetes, Order Moniliales, Family Tuberculariaceae. The disease is found all over the world wherever the crop of cotton is grown. It is believed that it has started from Mexico or Central America. The disease has been reported from many other countries such as Africa, France, Russia, Italy, and the West Indies. In India, the disease has been recorded from several states, especially Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat, and other parts of Central India.
