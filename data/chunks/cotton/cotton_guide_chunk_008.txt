IRRIGATION REQUIREMENTS AND SCHEDULING Cotton has a relatively deep root system and moderate drought tolerance compared to many field crops, but water stress at key growth stages significantly affects yield and fiber quality. Key considerations: - Establishment stage: Adequate soil moisture is needed at sowing and during germination/emergence for uniform stand establishment; both drought stress and waterlogging at this stage are damaging. - Vegetative and squaring stages: Moderate, well-managed irrigation supports healthy vegetative growth and square formation without promoting excessive vegetative growth at the expense of reproductive development; over-irrigation combined with high nitrogen at this stage can cause excessive vegetative growth ("rank growth") and delayed maturity. - Flowering and boll development: This is generally the most water-sensitive period for cotton; adequate, consistent moisture supply during flowering and boll filling is critical for maximizing boll retention, boll size, and fiber quality, and water stress during this window typically causes the greatest yield loss, including shedding of squares, flowers, and young bolls. - Boll maturation/opening stage: Irrigation is typically reduced or stopped as bolls mature and begin opening, since excess moisture at this stage can promote boll rot, poor fiber quality, and delayed, uneven boll opening; the final irrigation is usually timed to allow the field to dry sufficiently for picking.
