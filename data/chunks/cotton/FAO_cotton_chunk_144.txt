Success of infection is determined more by the plant condition than by the disease organism. Incubation: Incubation is the time required for an infection to cause symptoms. The development of symptoms is also a function of the plant type and condition, but also a function of the relative development rates of the plant and the disease. Disease is like a race between the plant and the disease organism. If the weather is better for the plant than for the disease (in terms of optimum temperature, water, sunlight, etc.) the plant may never show symptoms or only show minor symptoms. However, if weather conditions are best for the disease organism, the disease may quickly have symptoms, quickly develop inoculum that is ready to be moved to other leaves or plants. Inoculum development/reproduction: Production of fruiting bodies can be a function of temperature, sunlight and relative humidity. Movement of inoculum returns us to beginning of disease cycle.
