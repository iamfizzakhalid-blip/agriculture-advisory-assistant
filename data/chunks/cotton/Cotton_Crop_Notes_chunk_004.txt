This delay allows the root system to develop, since early irrigation would prevent roots from going deeper into the soil; it also restricts weed emergence. In total, 5–6 irrigations are given at intervals of 10–15 days. Thinning Thinning is carried out after germination, once the plants have reached a considerable height, to achieve the correct plant-to-plant distance. Diseased, weak, or poorly growing plants are removed during this process. Earthing Up Earthing up is done for flat-sown crops, after the second irrigation, either manually or mechanically, to avoid lodging. Hoeing Hoeing is performed 1–2 times before the first irrigation (dry hoeing) and again after irrigation (wet hoeing). Topping Topping is carried out before the bolls open. Insect Pests Cotton is affected by two categories of insect pests: Sucking pests: Aphid, Jassid, Thrips, and White fly Chewing pests: Army worm and Boll worms Cotton Varieties BT Varieties: Ali Akbar 703, Ali Akbar 806, Neelam 121, FH 114, IR 3, IR 1524, IR 3701, CIM 598, NS 141, Sitara 008, Sitara 009, MNH 886, A One, MG 6, Tarzan 1, and GN Hybrid 2085. Traditional Varieties: SLH 317, BH 167, FH 942, NIAB 852, NIBGE 115, CIM 496, CIM 506, CIM 554, CIM 573, MNH 786, CRSM 38, and SMH 151. Desi Varieties: Desi cotton accounts for about 10% of the total cotton area. It has a shorter staple length, coarser thread, and relatively higher ginning out-turn compared to other types. Common Desi varieties include Ravi and Rohi.
