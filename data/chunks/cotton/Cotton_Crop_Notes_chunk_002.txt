Egyptian cotton originated in Africa, American cotton in America, and Indian cotton in the subcontinent. These types originated separately at different times, and new varieties have since evolved through crossing. Adaptation Cotton requires frost-free conditions, ample sunlight, high temperatures, favorable moisture, and a sufficient water supply. The crop takes about 180–200 days to mature. Climate Requirements Germination: 15–18 °C Vegetative growth: 21–26 °C Flower bud initiation: 21 °C Reproductive stage: 26–32 °C Temperatures above 35 °C are not desirable Temperatures of 40–45 °C are managed through irrigation Soil Requirements Cotton can be grown in a wide range of soils, from sandy to heavy loam. Good drainage is important, and the crop is tolerant of salinity. Root Development Cotton has a tap root system that grows deeper when moisture is less available. Roots typically reach 3–4 metres deep, and sometimes 1–2 metres, and consist of primary and secondary roots. Branches Cotton has two types of branches. Sympodial branches are fruit-bearing and arise from monopodial branches. Monopodial branches are non-fruit-bearing and arise from the main stem. Leaf The cotton leaf is palmate, with lobes that vary in size and shape. Inflorescence Cotton has a complete flower that develops from floral buds enclosed in green sepals. When the petals open, they may appear white, pink, or dark maroon, transitioning from yellow to pink or violet. Boll Formation After the petals fall, the green sepals cover the immature boll. As the boll gains size, the sepals appear shorter, and their function ends once the boll opens. The boll contains locules, typically 4, 5, or 6 in number.
