In its systematic position, it belongs to Class Deuteromycetes, Order Sphaeropsidales, Family Sphaeropsidaceae. This disease occurs in several countries of the world, such as Venezuela, U. S. A., India, and parts of Africa. In India, the disease has been recorded from several states such as Madhya Pradesh, Karnataka, and Tamil Nadu. This fungus causes chiefly a seedling disease. Infected seedlings usually show a soft, yellow patch on the lower portion of the stem. In slightly older seedlings, when the stem is more woody, it becomes black at the ground level. On pulling out the infected plants, the entire root system may be found rotting and decaying. In bad cases, the rot is enough to cause tender plants to collapse, while older plants dry up and wither. The fungal sclerotia appear as minute black dots on the surface of woody tissue and bark. An infected root shows rounded sclerotia, and the fungal hyphae are thick-walled. The sclerotium and hyphae, as well as the pycnidium and its conidia, are characteristic features of the pathogen. The root rot of cotton is caused by Rhizoctonia bataticola (Taub.) Butl. The hyphae are formed in the rotted area, at first in the cortex, and later in bad cases extending up to the pith. The fungal hyphae are found inter- and intracellularly in the host tissue. The hyphae are septate and thick. The sclerotia are produced among the hyphae on the surface of the infected plant. Each sclerotium measures 100 microns in diameter. This is a soil-borne disease.
