SOWING METHOD, PLANT POPULATION, AND SEED RATE Cotton is typically sown in rows, either on flat ground or on ridges/beds, using a seed drill, planter, or manual dibbling depending on farm scale and mechanization level. - Row spacing varies by variety growth habit, region, and management system, but commonly falls in a range of roughly 60-100 cm between rows, with plant-to-plant spacing within the row adjusted to achieve the recommended plant population for the variety and region. - Optimum plant population balances yield potential against competition for light, water, and nutrients; both excessively low and excessively high plant populations can reduce yield - too low wastes land and light interception potential, while too high increases competition, vegetative growth at the expense of boll development, and disease/pest pressure due to a denser, more humid canopy. - Seed rate depends on seed size, germination percentage, spacing, and whether seed is delinted (fuzz removed) or fuzzy; delinted seed flows better through precision planters and is commonly used with mechanical planting. - Sowing depth is typically around 3-5 cm, adjusted for soil moisture and texture - slightly shallower in heavier, moisture-retentive soils and slightly deeper in lighter, drier soils to reach adequate moisture. - Seed treatment with an appropriate fungicide and/or insecticide before sowing helps protect germinating seedlings from soil-borne diseases (such as seedling blight/damping-off) and early-season sucking pests, and is a widely recommended practice. - Thinning of excess seedlings after emergence (where more than one seed per station is sown, or after broadcasting) helps establish the target plant population and reduces early competition among seedlings.
