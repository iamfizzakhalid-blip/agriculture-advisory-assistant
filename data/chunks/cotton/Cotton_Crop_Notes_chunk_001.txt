Cotton — Crop Production Notes Introduction and Local Names The scientific name of cotton is Gossypium hirsutum L., and it belongs to the family Malvaceae. In Urdu, cotton is called Kappas, and in Punjabi it is known as Phutti or Kappah. Species of Cotton Gossypium arboreum — Desi Cotton Gossypium hirsutum — American Cotton Gossypium barbadense — Egyptian Cotton Gossypium herbaceum — Indian Cotton Crop Characteristics Cotton is an annual, Kharif-season crop that is day neutral in nature. It is a dicot plant that is mostly self-pollinated, although about 20% cross-pollination occurs. The plant also has an indeterminate growth habit. Top Cotton Producing Countries The leading cotton-producing countries and their production figures (in tonnes), as recorded in the presentation, are as follows: China produced 17,910,000 tonnes, India produced 17,797,000 tonnes, the USA produced 9,473,800 tonnes, Pakistan produced 5,700,000 tonnes, and Turkey produced 1,272,800 tonnes. Cotton Production in Pakistan (2010) In 2010, the area under cultivation in Pakistan was 2,689,000 hectares, production was 5,700,000 tonnes, and the yield was 2.20 tonnes per hectare. In 2010, Pakistan ranked 4th in the world in cotton production. Importance of Cotton Cotton serves as the raw material for the textile industry and is also a source of vegetable/edible oil. Its seed cake is known as Banola. The crop is a significant source of foreign exchange and offers high returns to growers, which is why it is often referred to as "White Gold." More broadly, cotton is valued for five main uses, sometimes summarized as the five F's: Food Feed Fibre Fuel Forage Origin Cotton originated in the tropical and subtropical deserts and hot, dry regions of Asia, Australia, and America.
