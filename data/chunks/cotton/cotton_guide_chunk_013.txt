Management principles across cotton diseases: use of resistant or tolerant varieties suited to locally prevalent diseases, especially cotton leaf curl virus where endemic; use of certified, treated, disease-free seed; crop rotation with non-host crops to reduce soil-borne disease buildup, particularly important for wilt diseases that can persist in soil for years; control of insect vectors, particularly whitefly for viral diseases; balanced fertilization avoiding excessive nitrogen, which promotes dense, humid canopies favorable to fungal and bacterial disease development; and regular field monitoring to detect disease early. MAJOR COTTON PESTS 1. Bollworms (a complex of species including American bollworm/Helicoverpa armigera, pink bollworm/Pectinophora gossypiella, and spotted bollworm) - caterpillar pests that feed on squares, flowers, and bolls, causing direct damage to the yield-forming structures of the plant; among the most economically damaging cotton pests historically, driving the widespread adoption of Bt cotton varieties bred to express resistance to certain bollworm species, though pink bollworm in particular has developed resistance to some Bt traits in various regions over time, requiring integrated management approaches. 2. Whitefly (Bemisia tabaci) - a small sap-sucking insect that damages the plant directly through feeding and, more significantly, acts as the vector for cotton leaf curl virus in regions where the disease is present; whitefly populations tend to build up rapidly in hot, dry conditions and can also cause sooty mold development on honeydew excreted onto leaves, reducing photosynthesis. 3. Jassids/leafhoppers - sap-sucking insects that cause a characteristic yellowing/reddening and downward curling ("hopperburn") of leaf margins, reducing photosynthetic capacity and, in severe infestations, causing significant vegetative and yield loss. 4.
