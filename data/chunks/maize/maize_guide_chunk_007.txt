When it comes to actually sowing the crop, seed rate is usually somewhere around eight to ten kilograms per acre for typical dent hybrids at normal spacing, though this can vary depending on seed size, germination percentage, and how dense a stand the farmer is aiming for. A common spacing recommendation is around sixty to seventy five centimeters between rows and twenty to twenty five centimeters between individual plants within a row, though some hybrids are specifically bred to tolerate higher planting densities than this. Seeds are usually placed around three to five centimeters deep, going a bit deeper in dry or sandy soil to reach residual moisture, and a bit shallower in heavier or wetter soil. Sowing can be done by hand, with a seed drill, or with a precision planter, and precision planting tends to give more even emergence and spacing, which generally translates into better yields because the plants compete less with each other and use light and nutrients more efficiently. The exact timing of sowing depends heavily on the local climate and rainfall pattern, but the general goal is to time planting so that the critical flowering period does not coincide with the hottest or driest part of the season. Treating seed with a fungicide or insecticide before sowing is also common practice, since it protects the young seedling from soil borne diseases and early pests like termites or wireworms during the vulnerable germination period. Maize is a heavy feeder, and of all the nutrients it needs, nitrogen is the most critical and the one most likely to limit yield if it runs short. A total nitrogen application of around one hundred twenty to one hundred fifty kilograms per hectare is common
