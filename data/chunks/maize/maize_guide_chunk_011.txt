Stem borers are another common pest, with their larvae tunneling into the stalk, which in young plants can kill the central growing shoot, a symptom farmers often call dead heart, and in older plants can weaken the stalk enough that it breaks or falls over later in the season. Corn earworm, sometimes called cob borer, feeds directly on the developing kernels within the ear itself. Termites can damage roots and stems, especially in drier conditions, and aphids, besides sucking sap directly from the plant, can also spread viral diseases and, if they build up heavily on the tassels, can interfere with pollen shedding. Rodents and birds can also cause meaningful losses, particularly by eating newly sown seed before it germinates and by damaging ears as the crop approaches maturity. In general, an integrated approach that combines regular field scouting, resistant varieties, conservation of natural predators, and the use of pesticides only when pest numbers cross an economically damaging threshold tends to work better in the long run than routine blanket spraying. Diseases are another major factor in maize production. A number of fungal leaf diseases, including common rust and various leaf blights and leaf spots, show up as lesions, streaks, or raised pustules on the leaves, and severe infections reduce the amount of healthy leaf area available for photosynthesis, which directly cuts into yield; these are generally managed through resistant varieties, rotating maize with other crops, managing crop residue that can harbor disease, and fungicide application when infection is severe.
