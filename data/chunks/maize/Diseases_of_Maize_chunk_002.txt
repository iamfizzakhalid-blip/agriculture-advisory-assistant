Urediniospores are spread by wind over long distances, and the disease is polycyclic, meaning it has multiple infection cycles per season. Symptoms In the young plant or seedling stage, small, yellowish or chlorotic flecks appear on the leaves. These flecks enlarge into orange-brown powdery pustules. The pustules, known as uredia, are scattered on both leaf surfaces. Leaves may curl or become distorted. Severe infection causes yellowing and early leaf death, and the plant may show stunted growth if infection occurs early. In the old or adult plant, at the maturity stage, brick-red to cinnamon-brown elongated pustules appear on the leaves. Uredinia, which are summer spore masses, rupture the epidermis. Teliospores, which are dark brown to black, form in the late season. Telia form near or replace the uredia, appearing as dark streaks. Husks, sheaths, and stalks may also become infected. Premature drying of leaves leads to reduced grain filling. Management and Fungicides Cultural management measures include using certified, disease-free seed; early sowing to avoid the peak disease season; crop rotation with non-host crops; removing infected debris after harvest; and avoiding excessive nitrogen fertilizer. Recommended fungicides include Mancozeb 80%, from the Dithiocarbamate group, applied at 2.0 kg per hectare; Propiconazole, a Triazole (DMI) fungicide, applied at 0.5 L per hectare; Tebuconazole, a Triazole (DMI) fungicide, applied at 0.75 L per hectare; Azoxystrobin, a Strobilurin (QoI) fungicide, applied at 0.5 L per hectare; and Triadimefon, a Triazole fungicide, applied at 0.5 L per hectare. Regarding resistant varieties, growing rust-resistant maize hybrids is recommended, including Pioneer, Monsanto, and PARC approved varieties, and this is considered the best long-term control strategy.
