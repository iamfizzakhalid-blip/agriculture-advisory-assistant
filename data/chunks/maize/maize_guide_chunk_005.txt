Sandy soils, by contrast, drain too freely and tend to need more frequent irrigation and fertilizer application because they cannot hold water or nutrients as well. There are many different types of maize suited to different purposes. Dent corn is the most widely grown type worldwide and is mainly used for animal feed, starch production, and ethanol, and it gets its name from the dent that forms in the kernel as it dries. Flint corn has a harder, smoother kernel and is more traditionally grown in parts of Latin America and Europe. Sweet corn is harvested while still immature, at the milk stage, because at that point it has much higher sugar content and is eaten fresh or canned as a vegetable rather than being left to dry down like grain corn. Popcorn has a small, hard kernel with a tough outer layer that allows it to pop when heated, and flour corn has a soft starchy kernel that is easy to grind. There is also waxy corn, which has a different starch composition used for certain industrial purposes. Separately from these kernel types, farmers also choose between open pollinated varieties and hybrids. Open pollinated varieties allow farmers to save their own seed from one year to plant the next, and while they tend to yield somewhat less than hybrids, they are cheaper because seed does not need to be repurchased every season. Hybrid maize is created by crossing two or more specifically bred parent lines, and the resulting seed shows what is called hybrid vigor, meaning noticeably higher yield, more uniform plants, and often better tolerance to stress.
