Maize, also called corn and known scientifically as Zea mays, is a tall annual plant belonging to the grass family. It is a single-stemmed crop that usually grows between one and a half to three meters tall, depending on the variety and growing conditions. Maize is what is known as a C4 plant, which means it photosynthesizes very efficiently in hot, sunny weather and uses water more efficiently than crops like wheat or rice. This is part of why maize does so well in warm climates with plenty of sunlight. The plant has a fibrous root system, and as it grows taller it also develops brace roots, sometimes called prop roots, which emerge from the lower part of the stem above the soil surface and help anchor the plant so it does not fall over in wind or heavy rain. The stem is solid and divided into nodes and internodes, and the leaves are long and strap shaped, arranged alternately along the stem. At the very top of the plant is the tassel, which is the male flower structure and produces pollen. Lower down, growing out from a leaf joint, is the ear or cob, which is the female flower structure. The ear is wrapped in husk leaves, and from the tip of the ear, fine silk threads emerge; each individual silk is connected to one potential kernel on the cob, and that kernel can only develop if its silk receives a grain of pollen.
