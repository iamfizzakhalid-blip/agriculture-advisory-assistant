The leaves become covered with numerous uredosori, lose their green colour, dry up, and in severe cases the plant does not form cobs. The uredosori remain scattered or in groups on both surfaces of the leaves, and at first are like small yellow points, which elongate and sometimes coalesce, turn reddish-brown, and are first pustular. The pustules burst soon, dislodging the reddish-brown powdery spore-mass. The teleutosori appear later, as larger black patches, upto 2 mm long. They remain covered by the host epidermis but burst eventually. The rust of maize is caused by Puccinia sorghi Schw. The uredospores are elliptic or ovoid, light brown, finely spiny and measure 24 to 32 microns by 20 to 28 microns in diameter. Each uredospore possesses four germ pores. Two or more infection hyphae arise from the basal angles. The ordinary hyphae send haustoria into the host cells. The teleutospores are oblong, two-celled, thick-walled, slightly constricted at the septum, rounded or flattened at the apex and bright chestnut-brown in colour. They measure 28 to 48 microns by 13 to 25 microns and are borne on long, persistent, brownish stalks. The rust is a heteroecious one. The aecidial stage is being formed on species of Oxalis. The aeciospores are globose, pale yellow and finely verrucose. Rust Recurrence The inoculum survives in the form of uredospores on maize plants, which are generally grown round the year in different regions. By means of uredospores the disease cycle is repeated several times in the same season. Resistant varieties should be grown. Brown Spot of Maize The pathogen is Physoderma zeae-maydis Shaw, class Chytridiomycetes, order Chytridiales, family Cladochytriaceae.
