Regarding resistant varieties, growing rust-resistant maize hybrids is recommended, including Pioneer, Monsanto, and PARC approved varieties, and this is considered the best long-term control strategy. It is noted that fungicides should be applied at the first appearance of pustules, and the application should be repeated after 10 to 14 days if needed. Fungicide groups should be rotated to prevent resistance. Brown Spot of Maize (A Major Foliar Disease of Maize) Introduction Maize (Zea mays L.) is a globally important cereal crop. Brown Spot is a fungal disease affecting the leaves, sheath, and nodes of maize. It reduces the photosynthetic area and plant vigor of the crop. The disease is severe under warm, humid, and rainy conditions, and it causes significant yield and economic losses. Physoderma brown spot lesions appear in bands across the leaf surface, and yellowing bands appear on the corn leaf along with dark purple spots on the midrib. Causal Organism The pathogen causing Brown Spot of maize is Physoderma maydis. It is a soil- and residue-borne fungus that survives in crop debris and produces thick-walled resting spores. It is spread via rain splash and irrigation water. Taxonomic Classification Physoderma maydis belongs to the Kingdom Fungi, Phylum Blastocladiomycota, Class Blastocladiomycetes, and Genus Physoderma, with the species being P. maydis. Symptoms On the leaves, small yellow spots appear initially, which turn brown to dark brown. These spots are arranged in transverse bands across the leaf. The midrib and sheath are commonly infected, and in severe cases the leaves dry up.
