Over the course of a full season, maize typically needs somewhere between five hundred and eight hundred millimeters of water, though this varies depending on the variety's duration and the local climate, and the timing of that water matters enormously, since the plant can tolerate some dryness during its early vegetative growth but is very sensitive to water shortages during silking and during the grain filling period that follows. Because it is a C4 plant built for capturing sunlight efficiently, maize performs poorly in shade and generally needs full sun exposure to reach good yields. It is also frost sensitive at every stage of growth, so a late frost in spring or an early frost in autumn can both cause serious damage depending on when they occur relative to the crop's stage. In terms of soil, maize prefers deep, well drained loamy soils that are rich in organic matter, with an ideal soil pH somewhere between about five point eight and seven, which is to say slightly acidic to neutral. In soils that are more acidic than that, aluminum toxicity can become a problem and phosphorus becomes less available to the plant, so many farmers apply lime to correct very acidic soils before planting maize. Maize has quite poor tolerance for waterlogging, and even standing water for as little as one or two days during early growth can starve the roots of oxygen and kill a significant portion of the stand, so drainage is an important consideration, particularly on heavier clay soils.
