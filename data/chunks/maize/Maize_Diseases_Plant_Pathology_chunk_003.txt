The smut spores are globose or somewhat angular, brown coloured, and measure 10 to 16 microns in diameter, having numerous minute echines on their surface. Mingled with these smut spores are groups of hyaline, sterile cells, sub-spherical in shape and 7 to 15 microns in diameter. The enclosing membrane is formed of such cells. When young, the spores are aggregated in masses but at maturity, they are separated from each other. The smut spores germinate and produce 4-celled basidia. Sporidia are borne terminally and near the septa. The spores are shed from diseased plants on the soil around them and remain alive till the following season. The main infection comes from the soil and takes place usually during the early stage of seedling growth. Roguing: smutted plants should be removed as soon as they are detected. Crop rotation: rotation of the crops should be after two or three years. Resistant varieties: resistant varieties should be evolved. Rust of Maize The pathogen is Puccinia sorghi Schw., synonym P. maydis Bereng., class Basidiomycetes, order Uredinales, family Pucciniaceae. This rust is fairly common in South India. It is found abundantly under continuous relatively high temperature, as in South Europe, the warmer parts of the United States of America, Africa, and Australia. In recent times, it has been reported from many parts of India, especially infecting hybrid maize varieties. The rust does much harm, especially in thickly planted maize during periods of high humidity. The leaves become covered with numerous uredosori, lose their green colour, dry up, and in severe cases the plant does not form cobs.
