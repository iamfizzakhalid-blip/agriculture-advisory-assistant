Maize — Crop Production Notes Introduction and Local Names The scientific name of maize is Zea mays L., and it belongs to the family Poaceae. In Urdu, maize is called Makai, and in Punjabi it is known as Maki. Crop Characteristics Maize is an annual, short-day crop with a determinate growth habit. It is cross-pollinated, and its seed is referred to as a caryopsis. Origin Maize does not have an exact or specific origin, as it is a cross-pollinated crop. Synthetic maize, used mainly for fodder, has low yield potential, while hybrid maize, grown mainly for grain, has high yield potential. Both types originated from primitive maize. Top Maize Producing Countries The leading maize-producing countries, along with their production figures, were the USA with 316,165,000 tonnes, China with 177,540,788 tonnes, Brazil with 55,394,800 tonnes, Mexico with 23,301,900 tonnes, and Argentina with 22,676,900 tonnes. Maize Production in Pakistan (2016-17) In 2016-17, maize was cultivated on an area of 1.33 million hectares in Pakistan, with a total production of 6.130 million tonnes and a yield of 4.59 tonnes per hectare. Pakistan ranked 23rd in world maize production that year. Importance Maize is the third major cereal crop after wheat and rice. It is used to make bread, soups, and similar foods, and its grain is used as flour. It is also used to produce corn oil, popcorn, and energile. The grain contains about 85% carbohydrates and 5–7% oil. Maize is also used as fresh and dried fodder. Climate Requirements The minimum temperature required for maize is 10°C, while the optimum temperature range is 16–18°C. The crop is sensitive to very high temperatures, and leaf burn can occur in May–June due to hot winds.
