Dark spots occur particularly on the midrib of leaves, and lesions may appear in a characteristic band-like pattern across the leaf surface. On the stem and node, dark brown to black lesions appear on the nodes, causing weakening of the stalk tissue. This increases the risk of lodging and results in reduced plant productivity. Symptoms of Physoderma brown spot may also occur on the stalk, leaf sheath, and husks. Disease Identification In field diagnosis, numerous small brown spots are seen, arranged in a band-like pattern across the leaves, and the disease typically appears after rainy weather. In laboratory diagnosis, the presence of resting spores is observed, and microscopic confirmation of the pathogen is carried out. Environmental Requirements The disease favours a temperature range of 23 to 30 degrees Celsius, and a relative humidity above 80 per cent. It is favoured by frequent rainfall or irrigation, standing water in the leaf whorl, poor drainage, and a dense crop canopy. Susceptible Growth Stages At the seedling stage, the risk of infection is low. At the whorl stage, between V5 and V9, the crop shows the highest susceptibility. During the vegetative stage, the disease spreads rapidly. At the tasseling stage, infection is moderate. At maturity, symptoms persist but there is little new infection. Epidemiology The disease is favoured by warm, humid climates and is common in tropical and subtropical regions. It is spread by rain splash, irrigation water, and wind-driven droplets. Continuous maize cropping increases the risk of the disease, and water retention in the whorl enhances infection.
