Diseases of Field Crops Maize (Zea mays L.) Rust of Maize (Common Rust / Southern Rust) Rust of maize is caused by two related fungal pathogens: Common Rust, caused by Puccinia sorghi Schwein., and Southern Rust, caused by Puccinia polysora Underw. The host crop is maize (Zea mays L.). This is a fungal disease caused by a biotrophic fungus, and it is found worldwide, from tropical to temperate regions. It is considered a major yield-loss disease. The common name of the disease is Common Rust of Maize or Southern Rust of Maize, and it is also locally known as Corn Rust or Maize Rust. It is prevalent in tropical and subtropical regions. Causal Organism and Taxonomy Common Rust is caused by Puccinia sorghi Schwein., and Southern Rust is caused by Puccinia polysora Underw. Both pathogens belong to the Kingdom Fungi, Phylum Basidiomycota, Class Pucciniomycetes, Order Pucciniales, Family Pucciniaceae, and Genus Puccinia. The species differ, with Common Rust being P. sorghi and Southern Rust being P. polysora. Epidemiology Regarding temperature, the optimum for P. sorghi is 16 to 23 degrees Celsius, while P. polysora prefers 25 to 30 degrees Celsius. Sporulation stops below 10 degrees Celsius or above 35 degrees Celsius, and cool nights favour infection. Regarding humidity and moisture, the disease requires high relative humidity above 95 per cent, and free water or dew is needed for spore germination. A wet leaf surface for 6 to 8 hours promotes infection, and irrigation along with a dense canopy increases humidity. Regarding season and spread, in Pakistan the disease occurs from October to February, during the Rabi or cool season. The disease appears at the vegetative to silking stage.
