At the national level, cropland stress was estimated at about 1 percent in 2024, 8.6 percent in 2023, 4.2 percent in 2022, 17.8 percent in 2021, and 6.2 percent in 2025. Provincial data shows that Punjab, the leading maize-producing province, has generally experienced comparatively lower average stress levels than Balochistan and the northwestern regions, though flooding has periodically caused localized losses to standing maize crops, notably in 2022 and 2025. Other FAO/GIEWS Data Sources Referenced Additional detail on maize supply, utilization, and trade balances for Pakistan is available through the FAO/GIEWS Country Cereal Balance Sheet (CCBS). Price time series are available through the FAO/GIEWS Food Price Monitoring and Analysis (FPMA) Tool. Seasonal, vegetation, and precipitation maps, along with downloadable time-series data on the Agricultural Stress Index, NDVI, Vegetation Health Index, and precipitation, are available through FAO/GIEWS Earth Observation for Crop Monitoring. Food security phase classifications are available through the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) initiative. Broader long-run statistics on Pakistan's maize area, production, and yield are available through FAOSTAT and the FAO Country Profiles page for Pakistan.
