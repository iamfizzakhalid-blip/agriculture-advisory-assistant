Maize prefers high humidity and is more drought resistant at its early growth stages compared with the grain development stage. Soil Requirements Maize grows best in medium-textured, well-drained loam soils. It cannot be grown successfully in dry and sandy soils, and it is sensitive to salinity. Root Development Maize has an adventitious root system, along with aerial roots that provide additional support to the plant. Shoot Development The maize stem is jointed, with pithy internodes that are 2–4 cm in diameter. The stem is thicker at the base than at the upper side, and the base contains the roots. The leaves are arranged alternately along the stem. Inflorescence The tassel is the male part of the maize inflorescence and occupies the terminal position on the plant. The cob or ear is the female part and is located in the mid-portion of the stalk. The silk of the cob starts golden and turns brown, eventually drying and falling. Primary Tillage Primary tillage for maize involves 3–4 ploughings to break the hard pan, followed by 2–3 plankings and land levelling. Seed Bed Preparation Seed bed preparation involves 1–2 ploughings, use of a rotavator, 2 plankings, and the formation of ridges. Sowing Time The spring sowing period runs from mid-January to the end of February. The autumn, or seasonal, sowing period runs from the first week of July to 10th August, and early maturing varieties can be sown up to 20th August.
