Leaf Blight of Maize The pathogen is Helminthosporium turcicum Pass., class Deuteromycetes, order Moniliales, family Dematiaceae. This disease is quite common in many parts of India. It was first described in Italy in 1876, and since then has been recorded from the United States, South Africa, Japan, and most maize-growing countries. The symptoms appear as small, yellowish, round or oval spots on the leaves. These extend along the leaf and coalesce into longitudinal bands, which may cover a great part of the leaf. The affected tissues gradually become dark in colour, and are covered, in moist weather, with velvety dark-green patches due to fructifications of the fungus. Ultimately, the leaves dry up and have a blighted appearance. The plants remain stunted and the ears developed are poorly formed. The leaf blight of maize is caused by Helminthosporium turcicum Pass. The fungus grows both intra- and inter-cellularly within the mesophyll of the diseased spots. The conidiophores come out in groups from the stomata on the surface of the diseased spots. They are long, narrow, unbranched, slightly bent or curved above, septate, yellowish-brown, 150 microns long and 6 microns broad. The conidia are borne single at the tips and fall off readily. Conidia are olivaceous-brown, slightly curved, tapering towards the rounded ends, and vary in size and septation; usually they are 3 to 7 times transversely septate.
