FAO GIEWS — Pakistan Maize Compiled from the FAO/GIEWS Country Brief (current issue and archive), the Agricultural Stress Index dataset, and related FAO/GIEWS tools Current Situation (Reference Date: 6 February 2026) Maize crop production in 2025 is estimated at a slightly below-average level of 9.5 million tonnes. Low farm-gate prices of maize grain prompted some farmers to shift to more profitable vegetables and cash crops, including mustard and pulses, which contributed to this below-average output. Exports of maize in the 2025/26 marketing year (April to March) are forecast at an average level of 500,000 tonnes. Recent Production History Pakistan's maize production has grown substantially over the past two decades, driven by strong and sustained demand from the poultry and livestock feed industry, increasing adoption of hybrid seed varieties, and improved management practices. According to industry sources, maize production increased from about 1.66 million tonnes in 2001 to over 9.6 million tonnes in 2025. Punjab is the leading producing province, accounting for around 90 percent of total production, followed by Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. Selected officially estimated or forecast production figures reported over recent years include: about 4.5 to 4.8 million tonnes in 2014 (combining the spring and Kharif seasons); 4.6 million tonnes in 2015; around 4.9 to 5.2 million tonnes in 2016; 5.8 million tonnes in 2017 (above the previous year's high level, supported by increased plantings due to sustained feed industry demand); 7 million tonnes in 2020 (a record level at the time, reflecting a high level of plantings and increased productivity, with high-yielding seed varieties covering nearly 70 percent of the planted area); a preliminary forecast of 9.4 million tonnes for 2022 that required downward revision after flooding damaged standing Kharif crops; 10.3 to 11 million tonnes in 2023 (well above average, reflecting large sowings and high yields); 9.5 million
