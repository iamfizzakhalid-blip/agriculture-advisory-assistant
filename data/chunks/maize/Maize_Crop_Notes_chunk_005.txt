Post-Emergence Weed Control For the control of common broad leaf weeds and sedges, Primextra Gold 720SC herbicide is applied at 400–800 ml per acre after the first irrigation, when the field is moist, to effectively control these weeds. Harvesting Short duration maize varieties are harvested 90 days after sowing, while long duration varieties are harvested 120 days after sowing. Harvesting readiness is indicated when the cob changes color, the silk turns brown, and the grains become hard. After harvesting, cobs are stored in heaps for 3–4 days. Maize is harvested when the moisture content of the grains is between 20–25%. At this stage, the cob sheath dries completely, and some drying may also occur on the stalk. Cobs are removed from the standing crop and dried under sunlight until the grain moisture content falls below 15%. Shelling Hand-operated or powered maize shellers are preferred over manual shelling for the safe separation of dried maize grains from the cobs. After cleaning, shelled maize grains are stored in jute sacks in a cool, dry, and airy place, either on a cement floor or on wooden planks; storage on earthen floors should be avoided. The storage room temperature is kept at 20–25°C, with relative humidity maintained at 30–50%. Yield Maize hybrids are capable of producing more than 120 maunds of grain per acre, while local varieties typically yield 40–50 maunds per acre. Varieties Non-hybrid varieties include Sahiwal 2002 and MMRI Yellow. Hybrid Varieties Spring hybrids include Pioneer 3333, P 1543, Hicorn 8288, NK 8441, 33 M 15, NK 8711, and 32 F 10. Seasonal hybrids include 3025 Sufaid, 919, Hicorn 984, Hicorn 993, Hicorn 11, NK 6621, and 31 R 88.
