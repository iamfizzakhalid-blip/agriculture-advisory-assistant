Subsequent irrigations are applied at 7–10 day intervals in light soils and 10–15 day intervals in heavy soils. During flowering and fertilization, 2 days of dryness can cause a 20% yield reduction, while 1 week of dryness can cause a 50% yield reduction. For the spring crop, 11–12 irrigations are typically needed, while the Kharif crop requires 10–11 irrigations. The critical growth stages for irrigation are the seedling stage, grand growth stage, tasseling stage, silking stage, soft dough stage, and hard dough stage. Weed Management Methods Weed management methods fall into two types. Pre-sowing methods include crop rotation, the Dab method, soil solarization, and the use of herbicides. Post-sowing methods include hoeing, the use of herbicides, and the use of allelochemicals. Impact of Weeds Weeds can reduce maize yield by 29–43% or more. Competition between weeds and maize plants is at its maximum 2–5 weeks after crop emergence. Important Weeds of Maize Broad leaf weeds include Itsit, Bathu, Krund, Tandla, Lehli, Bakhra, Kulfa, Jangli Palik, Jangli Haloon, Chibber, Dodhak, and Chulai. Narrow leaf weeds, mainly grasses, include Swanki, Madhana, Khabbal, and Baru. Sedges are collectively known as Deela and include Cyperus rotundus, Cyperus iria, and Cyperus difformis. Pre-Emergence Weed Control Dual Gold, applied at 800 ml per acre, is used as a pre-emergence herbicide for the control of both narrow and broad leaf weeds. Post-Emergence Weed Control For the control of common broad leaf weeds and sedges, Primextra Gold 720SC herbicide is applied at 400–800 ml per acre after the first irrigation, when the field is moist, to effectively control these weeds.
