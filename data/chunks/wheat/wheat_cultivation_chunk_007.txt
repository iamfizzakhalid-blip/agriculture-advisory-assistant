Flowering (anthesis): Pollination occurs; this is a highly sensitive stage to heat stress, frost, and moisture stress. 8. Grain filling (milk and dough stages): Grain accumulates starch and protein, moving from a watery/milky consistency to a soft dough and then a hard dough; adequate moisture and nutrient supply during this stage strongly affect final grain weight. 9. Physiological maturity: The grain reaches maximum dry weight and the crop turns golden yellow; moisture content is still relatively high, but no further increase in grain weight occurs. 10. Harvest maturity: Grain moisture drops to a level suitable for mechanical harvesting and safe storage. IRRIGATION REQUIREMENTS AND SCHEDULING Wheat is sensitive to both water stress and waterlogging, and yield can be significantly reduced by moisture stress at critical growth stages. Key irrigation stages, in order of general importance, are: 1. Crown root initiation stage (about 20-25 days after sowing): Widely regarded as the single most critical irrigation, as it strongly affects root system development, tillering, and ultimately final yield; missing or delaying this irrigation is one of the most damaging management errors in wheat production. 2. Tillering stage (about 40-45 days after sowing): Supports tiller development and canopy establishment. 3. Jointing/stem elongation stage: Supports rapid vegetative growth and ear development within the stem. 4. Flowering/heading stage: Water stress at this stage can cause floret sterility and reduced grain set. 5. Milk stage (early grain filling): Important for establishing grain number and initial grain size. 6. Dough stage (late grain filling): Affects final grain weight and test weight; water stress here causes shriveled, lightweight grain.
