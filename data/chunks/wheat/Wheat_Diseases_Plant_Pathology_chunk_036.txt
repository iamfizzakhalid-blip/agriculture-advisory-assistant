Fungicidal sprays proved effective in control of this disease: spray with systemic fungicide, benlate (0.1%) gave good results. Spray of calixin is equally effective. Karathane (0.1%) is also effective. Control to some extent can also be achieved with ethirimol and dimethirimol, used on soil drench. Seed treatment with bayleton (0.1-0.2%) or its spray on leaves (100-500 µg/ml) gave good results. Leaf Blight of Wheat The pathogen is Alternaria triticina Prasada and Prabhu., class Deuteromycetes, order Moniliales, family Dematiaceae. This disease causes much loss in various parts of India. This disease was first observed in 1924, and since then the plant pathologists are busy in various investigations. Several rust-resistant varieties have been proved quite susceptible for this disease. The symptoms of the disease are not visible on the seedlings, and they appear only when the plants become 7-8 week old. Durum wheat has been found to be most susceptible for this disease (Prasada and Prabhu, 1962). The disease has not been reported from other countries so far. In early stages, small, oval or elliptical, irregular, hyaline lesions are found to be scattered on the leaves. On maturity, these lesions become quite irregular and brown or grey-coloured. The lesions remain surrounded by a bright yellow zone. Several lesions coalesce and cover most of the surface of the leaf, ultimately causing its death. Sometimes the leaves begin to dry up from their apices. In moist conditions, the lesions are covered with black powdery conidia of the fungus. The lowermost leaves of the plants which are nearer to the irrigation channels get infected first. The symptoms of the disease appear on leaf sheath, leaf blade, ears, awns and glumes.
