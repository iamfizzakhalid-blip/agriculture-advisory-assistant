In humid atmosphere, the yellow slime can be seen trickling down, but when it dries up, it becomes sticky and afterwards hard and dry. Ultimately the spikes become curled and twisted. The bacterium cannot cause the disease, and it is always associated with the nematode, Anguina tritici. In association with the nematode, the kernels are replaced by nematode galls and yellow rot sets in. The yellowing rot or tundu disease of wheat is caused by the bacterium Corynebacterium rathayi (Smith) Dowson, synonym Corynebacterium tritici and the nematode, Anguina tritici (Steinbach) Filipjev. The bacterium is a motile rod with one polar flagellum. It measures 2-3 by 1 µ. In most of the infected grains the kernels are replaced by nematode galls and these galls carry the causal bacterium. This is a seed-borne disease. If the infected seeds are sown, the galls absorb moisture from the soil, and the larvae of the nematodes are released out from the ecto-parasites on the growing apex of the seedlings. At the time of flowering, the ovaries of the flowers are being infected by the nematodes already present on the growing apex. Once again the galls are formed. In the mean time, the bacterial infection also takes place and the yellow ear rot sets in. Control Measures (Diseases Management) Proper drainage: it has been suggested that if the fields are well-drained, there are less chances of epidemic. Sanitation: as soon as the symptoms appear, the plants should be uprooted and destroyed by burning them, out of the field. Sowing of clean seeds: the gall-free seeds should be sown.
