The summer heat in the plains that follows the wheat harvest kills all the uredospores of the rusts in the next season. Consequently there is no local source of infection when the crop is sown in the plain in the next season. On the other hand, in the hills, all the three rusts have been found abundantly on stubbles, self-sown wheat and out of season crops at different altitudes ranging from 4,000 to 8,000 feet above sea-level. Crops in the hills get infected early in the neighbourhood of these of self-sown volunteer wheat plants and the spores are then disseminated and carried down by the air currents to the foot hills where they cause infection to the crop present there, and from this crops in the plains get infected. In general, rusts appear in places at the foot hills than those situated farther away in the plains. Spores of all these three rusts have been caught on aeroscope slides at a larger number of stations in the plains before the appearance of the rusts showing clearly that the spores of rusts are introduced in the plains by wind coming either from the hills or passed over areas where rust had appeared earlier. In addition to over summering in the hills on self-sown wheat plants, two important foci exist according to Mehta where due to early crop there is plenty of rust every year at the time wheat is sown in the neighbouring plains. These are Central Nepal in the North and the Nilgiri and Palini hills in the South.
