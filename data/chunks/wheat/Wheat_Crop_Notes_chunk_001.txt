Wheat — Crop Production Notes Introduction and Local Names The scientific name of wheat is Triticum aestivum L., and it belongs to the family Poaceae. In Urdu, wheat is called Gandum, in Punjabi it is known as Kanak, and in Pashto it is called Ghanum. Crop Characteristics Wheat is an annual, long-day crop with a determinate growth habit. It is self-pollinated, its seed is referred to as grain, and its pollination takes place on the spike. History According to tradition, wheat is associated with Hazrat Adam (A. S.) and the heavens, and with the Fertile Crescent and the Nile Delta. The first domestication of wheat is believed to have taken place in southeastern Turkey around 9,000 B. C., while pre-domesticated wheat dates back to around 23,000 B. C. The Fertile Crescent is a crescent-shaped region containing the comparatively moist and fertile land of otherwise arid and semi-arid Western Asia, along with the Nile Valley and Nile Delta of northeast Africa. Top Wheat Producing Countries According to FAO (2019) data, China led wheat production with 134,340,630 tonnes, followed by India with 98,510,000 tonnes, Russia with 85,863,132 tonnes, the USA with 47,370,880 tonnes, and France with 36,924,938 tonnes. Wheat Production in Pakistan (2019) In 2019, wheat was cultivated on an area of 9,200,000 hectares in Pakistan, with a total production of 27,000,000 tonnes and a yield of 2.93 tonnes per hectare. Pakistan ranked 8th in world wheat production that year. Morphology Wheat has a hypogeal mode of germination and a fibrous, adventitious root system. The root depth is normally about 60 cm, but it can extend up to 200 cm. Shoot Development The shoot develops from the plumule, and many tillers can arise from a single seed.
