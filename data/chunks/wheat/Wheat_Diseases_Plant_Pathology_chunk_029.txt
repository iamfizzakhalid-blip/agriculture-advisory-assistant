The interior of the grain is filled with a greasy spore-mass, smelling strongly of rotten fish. The spore-masses entirely replace the grain contents while leaving the grain coats intact. The spores are hard and it ruptures at some time during threshing, and then the spores adhere to the healthy grains. Rough-spored bunt or stinking smut of wheat is caused by Tilletia caries (DC.) Tul. The spores possess rough and reticulate wall, measuring 15-20 microns in diameter. On germination, the spore produces a stout promycelium (basidium). At the tip of promycelium, a cluster of 4 to 12 long, narrow, curved sporidia is formed. The sporidia usually become united in pairs by lateral branches forming H-shaped structures. Secondary sickle-shaped sporidia are produced on the germination of H-shaped structure. The infection can take place only when the germ tube of a primary or secondary sporidium comes in contact with a young seedling. The disease is seed-borne, and the infection takes place through seed-borne spores. After infection (by means of sporidia) the fungus grows along the tip of the shoot, and at the time of flowering, the fungal hyphae concentrate in the floral parts, transforming the grain into smutted tissues, enclosing masses of bunt spores. The spore-mass can survive for more than 25 years under dry conditions. Resistant varieties: for effective and economical control the resistant varieties should be grown. Some resistant varieties are: PV 18, S227, HD 2012, HD 4513, HD 4519, HB 383, WL 410, WL 885, WL 1581, IWP 72, IWP 87, IWP, 127, IWP 129, etc. Seed treatment: since the disease is externally seed-borne, the seeds should be treated with specific chemicals such as sulphur, copper carbonate and organo-mercurials before sowing.
