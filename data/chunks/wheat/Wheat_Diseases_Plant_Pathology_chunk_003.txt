On getting suitable conditions of moisture and temperature, the uredospores germinate producing germ tubes. The uredospores germinate on the epidermis of the host, developing into a new parasitic mycelium within the plant. A second germ tube is often formed directly, but soon ceases growth, but the tip swells up into an elongated appressorium, measuring about 27 by 29 microns. From the appressorium, a narrow branch passes into the stoma and immediately swells up to form a sub-stomatal vesicle, all the contents of the germ tube collect under this vesicle, and soon after a hypha arises from one end and grows towards a neighbouring cell, into which it sends a haustorium. Infection is now complete and the mycelium grows continuously within the plant. From this mycelium, new pustules are formed within the plant. From this mycelium, new pustules are formed within 10 to 15 days from infection, and thousands of uredospores are formed and liberated out into the air. A single pustule contains hundreds of spores. Each spore is capable of causing a new infection and new pustules; it becomes quite clear how the fungus can multiply and spread through a field, until every plant is infected. The teleuto-sori (telia) develop on the same mycelium, in the last part of the season. The teleutospores differ from the uredospores in being firmly fixed on their stalks, and composed of two cells with a thick smooth wall.
