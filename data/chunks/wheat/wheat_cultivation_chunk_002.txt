Wheat grain consists of the bran (outer layer, rich in fiber), the endosperm (starchy interior, milled into white flour), and the germ (embryo, rich in oil, protein, and micronutrients). TYPES AND CLASSIFICATION OF WHEAT - Bread wheat (Triticum aestivum): The most widely grown type, used for bread, chapati, and general flour products; hexaploid. - Durum wheat (Triticum durum): Hard wheat with high protein and gluten strength, primarily used for pasta and semolina; tetraploid. - Winter wheat: Sown in autumn, requires a period of cold temperature (vernalization) to induce flowering, harvested in early-mid summer. - Spring wheat: Sown in spring, does not require vernalization, harvested in late summer/autumn; grown where winters are too severe for winter wheat to survive. - Classification by grain hardness and color: hard red winter, hard red spring, soft red winter, hard white, soft white, and durum, each suited to different end uses (bread, pastry, noodles, pasta). CLIMATE AND SOIL REQUIREMENTS Wheat grows best in a cool, moist climate during the vegetative growth period and a warm, dry climate during ripening and harvesting. Optimal temperature for germination is around 20-25°C; growth can occur between roughly 3-4°C (minimum) and 30-32°C (maximum), with the ideal range for most growth stages being 15-20°C. Excessively high temperatures (above roughly 30-32°C), particularly during flowering, grain filling, and ripening, cause shriveled grain, reduced grain weight, and yield loss. Frost during flowering can also damage the crop. Adequate but not excessive rainfall or irrigation is required; total water requirement is generally in the range of 400-650 mm depending on climate, evapotranspiration, and variety. Wheat can be grown on a wide range of soils but performs best on well-drained loamy or clay-loam soils with good water-holding
