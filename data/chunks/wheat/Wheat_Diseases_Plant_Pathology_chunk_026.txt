The spores germinate by producing a promycelium (basidium) or 1-3-celled germ-tube, which bears a cluster of 2 to 6 sporidia at the apex. The sporidia (basidiospores) measure 12-15 by 3 to 2 microns and germinate without falling off by a slender germ-tube. The disease is both seed-borne and soil-borne. Infection occurs below ground. The spore balls are adapted to retain their power of germination for a long time. On germination they infect the primary shoot and later produce the characteristic spore bearing sori. Resistant varieties: the sowing of resistant varieties is most effective method of control. Some resistant varieties are: NP 165, WG 189, VL 426, HD 2117, HW 161, HB 121, HB 113, etc.; recommended for cultivation in Punjab. Seed treatment: the seeds should be treated with sulphur or organo-mercurials before sowing. They reduce the primary infection. Crop rotation: this practice has also been effective in controlling this disease. Smooth-spored Bunt of Wheat The pathogen is Tilletia foetida (Wallr.) Liro., class Basidiomycetes, order Ustilaginales, family Tilletiaceae. In India, this disease is commonly found in the hilly regions at elevations of 6,000 feet and above. The plants affected by this disease ripen a little earlier than the healthy plants. The affected ears become dark green in colour and are more open than those of the ears of healthy plants. The affected ears become narrower and longer than the healthy ones. On the maturity of the ears the glumes are pushed apart. The affected grains become shorter than the healthy ones. As a rule, all the ears in an affected plant become infected. All the grains of an ear are being converted into spore balls.
