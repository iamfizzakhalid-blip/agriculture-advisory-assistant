Wheat area planted has also varied: it stood at about 8 million hectares for the 2018/19 season (9 percent below the prior year due to low irrigation water availability); around 9.2 million hectares for the 2020/21 and 2021 seasons; 9.5 million hectares for the 2023/24 season; large sowings supporting the record 2024 output; and a decline of 6.5 percent year-on-year for the 2025 season following the removal of the minimum support price, before area planted returned to an above-average level again for the 2026 season. Trade History Pakistan is traditionally a wheat exporter, but it has imported significant quantities of wheat in years of tight domestic supply. Reported import figures include: about 900,000 tonnes in the 2013/14 marketing year; roughly 500,000 to 800,000 tonnes in 2014/15; a forecast decline to 250,000 tonnes for 2015/16; approximately 1.7 million tonnes in 2020/21, the largest volume in 12 years at that time; 1.8 million tonnes in 2021/22; 2.3 million tonnes in 2022/23; 1.8 million tonnes in 2023/24; a forecast of negligible imports for 2024/25 following the record 2024 harvest, with actual imports around 100,000 tonnes; and a forecast of about 1.1 million tonnes for 2025/26, significantly below the five-year average, though an import ban introduced in July 2024 and still in effect as of January 2026 may keep actual imports even lower than this forecast. Wheat exports have also varied by season: reported figures include around 700,000 to 850,000 tonnes across several seasons between 2016/17 and 2018/19, and a sharp drop to about 200,000 tonnes in 2019/20 following a government export ban introduced in September 2019 to protect domestic availability.
