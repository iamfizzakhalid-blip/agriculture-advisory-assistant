Loose Smut of Wheat The pathogen is Ustilago nuda (Jens.) Rostrup., class Basidiomycetes, order Ustilaginales, family Ustilaginaceae. It is fairly common disease in most of the wheat growing tracts of India, without often becoming epidemic or doing much damage. Sometimes the disease causes severe damage, destroying about 30 per cent ears of some infected fields. Its distribution is throughout the world. This disease is of common occurrence in western districts of Uttar Pradesh. The symptoms of the disease may be seen only on the emergence of the ears. The black powder can be seen only in the ear instead of flowers. All the parts of the spikelet are being converted into smut spores except awns. In the beginning, the smut sori remain covered with thin silvery membrane, but soon after this membrane disintegrates and the smut spores are liberated in the atmosphere. These spores are blown off by wind and other agencies and the rachis remains barren. The loose smut of wheat is caused by Ustilago nuda (Jens.) Rostrup.
