In India, resistant varieties of wheat have been developed. They are: IWP 129, IWP 127, IWP 72, PV 18, HD 2012, H 4513, HD 4519, Kalyan Sona, UP 2002, HB 383, WL 410, WL 885, WL 1581, etc. Seed treatment: the disease has been effectively controlled by seed treatments. Prior to sowing the seeds are treated with specific chemicals such as copper carbonate, ceresan, agrosan GN and other such chemicals. Rough-spored Bunt or Stinking Smut of Wheat The pathogen is Tilletia caries (DC.) Tul., class Basidiomycetes, order Ustilaginales, family Tilletiaceae. In India, this bunt occurs only in regions with a low temperature and a cool climate and at 6,000 feet elevation above sea level. Sometime ago the disease has also been reported from Haryana state. The disease is commonly found in Kashmir, the Western Himalayas and Himachal Pradesh. This is much more serious disease of wheat in the countries in which it occurs, than the loose smut. The symptoms are seen only on the ears. Affected plants ripen usually more rapidly than healthy plants and the ears become of a dark, green colour and more open. The fully mature grain becomes dark and the dark colour can be seen between the glumes, which are more spread out than usual. Instead of bending over like the healthy ears, they remain erect and rigid, instead of bending over like the healthy ears. The interior of the grain is filled with a greasy spore-mass, smelling strongly of rotten fish. The spore-masses entirely replace the grain contents while leaving the grain coats intact. The spores are hard and it ruptures at some time during threshing, and then the spores adhere to the healthy grains.
