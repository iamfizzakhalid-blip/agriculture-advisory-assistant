Seed treatment to check seedling infection has been recommended. Copper sulphate solution (2%), formalin (500 g/litre of water) and copper carbonate dust (2-3 oz/30 kg seed) have been recommended. Organo-mercurials, such as agrosan GN, NI ceresan and ceresan M have also been recommended for seed treatment. Seed treatment with benlate (0.3%) and vitavax (0.2%) is also recommended. Triazole and bitertanol @ of 19 µm/kg seed are found very effective for seed treatment. Karnal Bunt of Wheat The pathogen is Tilletia barclayana Sacc. and Sydow., class Basidiomycetes, order Ustilaginales, family Tilletiaceae. In India, for the first time this disease was reported from Karnal (then in the Punjab, now in Haryana), and since then this has been named as 'Karnal bunt of wheat'. In Uttar Pradesh, this disease was first reported in 1942, and since then it has been reported from several districts of Western Uttar Pradesh. Elsewhere in the country this disease is unknown. Though the disease is of less importance, yet in certain years it caused heavy damage. This disease can be observed in the fields only when the ears have emerged out, and grains are formed. In an affected plant, not all the ears are infected. On the other hand, in an infected ear, only a few grains not more than five or six, are being infected and changed into sori. The sori are always irregularly distributed on the infected ear. On the maturity of the grains, the outer glumes are changed into sori.
