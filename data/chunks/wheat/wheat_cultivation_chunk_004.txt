WHEAT VARIETIES AND SEED SELECTION Wheat varieties differ in maturity duration, yield potential, disease resistance, lodging resistance, grain quality, and adaptation to local climate and soil. Key factors farmers should weigh when selecting a variety include: - Disease resistance, especially to the locally prevalent rust races and smuts, since new rust strains periodically overcome resistance in older varieties. - Maturity duration (early, medium, or late maturing), which affects sowing/harvest windows and suitability for the following crop in rotation. - Yield potential under local management and irrigation conditions. - Lodging resistance, particularly important where high nitrogen and irrigation are used. - Response to fertilizer and irrigation (some varieties are bred specifically for high-input, high-yield systems). - Grain quality traits relevant to end use (protein content, gluten strength, grain hardness, milling and baking quality). - Tolerance to heat stress (important for late-sown crops or regions with rising terminal heat) and tolerance to salinity or drought where relevant. Farmers should select varieties recommended by local agricultural research institutes or extension services for their specific agro-climatic zone, since varieties are regionally tested. Using certified seed from a reliable, reputable source rather than farm-saved seed from previous harvests is strongly recommended, as certified seed has higher genetic purity, higher germination rates, and is free of seed-borne diseases such as loose smut and Karnal bunt. Seed should also be tested for germination percentage before sowing; a germination rate below about 85% suggests a higher seed rate is needed or the seed lot should be replaced.
