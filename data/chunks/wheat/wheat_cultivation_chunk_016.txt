In some systems, wheat follows a summer crop such as rice or cotton in the same field within a single year, requiring careful timing of harvest of the preceding crop and land preparation to allow wheat to be sown within its optimum window. MECHANIZATION IN WHEAT PRODUCTION Modern wheat production increasingly relies on mechanization at multiple stages: tractor-drawn plows, disc harrows, and cultivators for land preparation; seed-cum-fertilizer drills for precise, uniform row sowing and simultaneous basal fertilizer placement; laser land levelers for precision leveling that improves irrigation efficiency and crop uniformity; sprayers for herbicide, fungicide, and foliar fertilizer application; and combine harvesters for efficient, timely harvesting and threshing, particularly important in regions where a narrow window exists between wheat harvest and the sowing of the following crop. Mechanization reduces labor requirements and turnaround time between crops, and precision equipment such as laser levelers and seed drills can measurably improve both input use efficiency and yield compared to traditional manual methods. HARVESTING Wheat is ready for harvest at physiological maturity, when the crop turns from green to golden yellow and the grains become firm and hard, having reached their maximum dry weight; at this point, further ripening mainly involves loss of moisture rather than any increase in yield.
