SOWING TIME The optimum sowing time is generally from late October to November in most temperate and subtropical wheat-growing regions, though exact timing depends on local climate, latitude, and the specific variety's maturity type and vernalization requirement. Sowing too early can expose the crop to warmer temperatures that encourage excessive vegetative growth, higher pest pressure (such as aphids and termites), and increased disease risk before winter. Sowing too late (after the optimum window) shortens the vegetative and tillering period, reduces the number of productive tillers, and exposes the crop to high temperatures during flowering and grain filling in spring, which sharply reduces yield through poor grain filling and shriveled grain. Where late sowing is unavoidable, farmers should switch to a variety specifically bred for late sowing/heat tolerance and increase the seed rate to compensate for reduced tillering. SOWING METHOD AND SEED RATE Wheat can be sown by broadcasting, drilling with a seed drill, dibbling, or bed planting (raised beds with furrows for irrigation). - Line/drill sowing in rows is generally preferred over broadcasting because it ensures uniform seed depth and spacing, better and more even germination, easier interculture and weed control, and more efficient irrigation. - Recommended row-to-row spacing is typically about 20-22.5 cm, though closer spacing (15-18 cm) may be used for late-sown crops to compensate for reduced tillering. - Seed should be sown at a depth of about 4-5 cm; sowing too deep delays emergence, weakens seedlings, and reduces final stand, while sowing too shallow risks poor anchorage and exposure to drying. - Standard seed rate is generally around 100-125 kg per hectare for normal, timely sowing, with a somewhat higher rate (up to about
