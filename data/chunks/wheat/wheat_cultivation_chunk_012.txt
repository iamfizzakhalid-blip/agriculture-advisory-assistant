An internally seed-borne disease that destroys the entire ear, replacing the grain with a black powdery mass of spores that is dispersed by wind, leaving only the bare rachis; spreads mainly through infected seed, making seed treatment and use of certified disease-free seed the primary control method. - Karnal bunt - caused by Tilletia indica. A seed- and soil-borne disease that partially converts individual grains into black spore masses, imparting a characteristic fishy odor to the grain; primarily a grain-quality and trade/marketability issue rather than a major yield-reducing disease, but it can lead to rejection of grain lots for milling or export. 3. Foliar fungal diseases: - Powdery mildew - caused by Blumeria graminis f. sp. tritici. Appears as white to grayish powdery fungal growth on leaf surfaces, leaf sheaths, and sometimes the ear; favored by dense crop canopies, high humidity, and excessive nitrogen application; reduces photosynthetic area and can lower yield if severe. - Septoria leaf blotch (Septoria tritici blotch and Stagonospora nodorum blotch) - causes irregular brown to tan lesions, often with small dark fungal fruiting bodies visible within the lesions, on leaves; reduces the green leaf area available for grain filling, and severe infection can significantly reduce yield and grain quality. - Tan spot - caused by Pyrenophora tritici-repentis, produces tan/brown lesions often with a yellow halo, particularly common where wheat is grown in continuous rotation with wheat residue left on the soil surface (favoring reduced/no-till systems without proper residue management). 4. Root and crown diseases: Various soil-borne fungi (such as Fusarium and Bipolaris species) can cause seedling blight, root rot, and crown rot, particularly under moisture stress, poor drainage, or continuous wheat cropping without rotation. Management principles across wheat diseases: use of resistant or tolerant varieties as the first line
