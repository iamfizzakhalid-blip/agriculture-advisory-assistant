WEED MANAGEMENT Common weeds competing with wheat include wild oats (Avena fatua and Avena ludoviciana), littleseed canary grass (Phalaris minor), other grassy weeds, and broadleaf weeds such as Chenopodium album (lamb's quarters), Convolvulus arvensis (field bindweed), and Rumex species (docks). Weeds compete directly with wheat for nutrients, water, light, and space, and can cause substantial yield loss if not controlled, especially during the first 30-45 days after sowing, which is the most critical weed-competition window. Weed management approaches include: - Thorough pre-sowing field preparation to physically remove weed roots, rhizomes, and reduce the weed seed bank. - Use of clean, weed-free certified seed to avoid introducing new weed seeds into the field. - Crop rotation with different crop types (e.g., rotating with rice, cotton, or legumes) to break the life cycle of wheat-specific weeds such as Phalaris minor, which becomes harder to control under continuous wheat monocropping. - Timely first irrigation combined with manual or mechanical weeding at around 30-35 days after sowing, since early weed removal has the largest impact on preserving yield. - Herbicide application using selective herbicides appropriate for the specific weed spectrum present (grassy weed herbicides versus broadleaf herbicides, or combination products), applied at the correct crop and weed growth stage as recommended by local agricultural experts, to maximize efficacy and avoid crop injury. - Herbicide resistance management, including rotating herbicide modes of action and integrating non-chemical control methods, is increasingly important in regions where weeds such as Phalaris minor have developed resistance to commonly used herbicides after repeated use.
