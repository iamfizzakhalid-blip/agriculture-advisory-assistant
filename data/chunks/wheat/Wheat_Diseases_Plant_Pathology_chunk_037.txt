In moist conditions, the lesions are covered with black powdery conidia of the fungus. The lowermost leaves of the plants which are nearer to the irrigation channels get infected first. The symptoms of the disease appear on leaf sheath, leaf blade, ears, awns and glumes. This disease is caused by Alternaria triticina Prasada and Prabhu. The mycelium of this fungus is hyaline in early stage but later on, it becomes olive buff in appearance. The conidiophores are branched, septate, and 2-7 microns broad. The conidiophores are usually unbranched, rarely branched, erect, single or in groups emerging out through the stomata, the conidiophores measure 17-28 by 3-6 microns. The conidia may develop on the conidiophores either singly or in the chains of 2-4. The conidia are smooth, irregularly oval, elliptical or ellipsoid and beaked. The beaks are elongated and pointed towards their ends. The beak is of the same colour of which the main body of the conidium. The conidium is light brown or dark olive-brown in colour. Each conidium measures 15-89 by 7-30 microns. Each conidium possesses 1-10 transverse septa and 0-5 longitudinal septa. The conidia germinate between 5°C to 35°C. The optimum temperature for germination varies from 15°C-27°C. Maximum germination needs 100 per cent relative humidity. Maximum incidence of disease requires 25°C temperature. This is a seed- as well as soil-borne disease. The intensity of the disease varies on different varieties and on an average it is 12.2 per cent. The inoculum is found to be deep seated in the seed. Seven to fifteen days old seedlings are not susceptible. As the plants become older, they become more susceptible to this disease.
