FAO GIEWS — Pakistan Wheat Compiled from the FAO/GIEWS Country Brief (current issue and archive), the Agricultural Stress Index dataset, and related FAO/GIEWS tools Current Situation (Reference Date: 6 February 2026) The area planted with the 2026 wheat crop in Pakistan is estimated to be above the five-year average. Planting of the mostly irrigated 2026 wheat crop was finalized in December 2025, and harvesting is expected to start in mid-March 2026. The larger planted area was supported by near-record domestic prices of wheat flour and adequate soil moisture conditions at planting time. Farmers were encouraged to plant wheat by government support measures, including the distribution of high-yielding seed varieties and an announcement in October 2025 that government procurement of wheat would resume for the 2026 crop after a two-year suspension, with planned purchases of about 2.5 to 3 million tonnes of wheat grain at the minimum support price. Despite below-average precipitation between October and mid-December 2025, vegetation conditions were average to above average in the main wheat cropping areas due to a sufficient supply of irrigation water. Dry weather caused some crop losses in Barani (rainfed) areas, located mostly in the northern parts of Punjab province, which account for about 20 percent of total wheat plantings. Precipitation in these areas returned close to average during the third dekad of December and the third dekad of January, helping replenish soil moisture and support crop growth. As of late January 2026, remote sensing data indicated satisfactory vegetation conditions in most parts of the country. Overall, 2026 wheat production will depend on the performance of the rainy season through April 2026, since abundant rainfall is needed both to support crop development and to replenish the main reservoirs used for irrigating the 2026 main-season crops from May onward.
