Overall, 2026 wheat production will depend on the performance of the rainy season through April 2026, since abundant rainfall is needed both to support crop development and to replenish the main reservoirs used for irrigating the 2026 main-season crops from May onward. Recent Production History Pakistan's wheat production has fluctuated year to year depending on planted area, weather, irrigation water availability, fertilizer supply, and government pricing policy. Selected officially estimated or forecast production figures reported over recent years include: about 25 million tonnes in 2014; 25.5 million tonnes in 2015 (2 percent below the 2014 level but 4 percent above the five-year average); around 25.5 to 25.9 million tonnes in 2016; 25.8 million tonnes in 2017 (marginally above the 2016 level); 25.4 million tonnes in 2018 (about 4 percent below the previous year's bumper crop); 25.25 million tonnes in 2020 (a below-average outcome reflecting reduced yields due to heavy rains and hail in Punjab, as well as locust infestation); 27 million tonnes in 2021 (a near-record level); 26.1 million tonnes in 2022 (a near-average level, with yields affected by heatwaves and shortages of urea fertilizer and irrigation water); 28.1 million tonnes in 2023 (above average); 31.4 million tonnes in 2024 (a record level, reflecting large sowings and excellent yields); and 29 million tonnes in 2025, which was slightly above the average level but 8 percent lower year-on-year than 2024, mainly reflecting a contraction in area planted due to low domestic prices at planting time and the removal of the minimum support price.
