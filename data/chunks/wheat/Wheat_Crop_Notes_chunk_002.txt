The stem has pithy nodes and hollow internodes. Spike The wheat spike is made up of spikelets, each containing three florets, also called glumes. Each floret has two parts: the lemma and the palea. The lemma is the outer covering and contains the awn, while the palea lies beneath the lemma and covers the grain. Climate Requirements Wheat is a winter, or Rabi, crop that requires low temperatures of about 4–10°C. Tillering takes place over about 1.5 months, from December to January. In snowy areas, wheat is sown either after the snow melts (April–May) or before snowfall (October–November). Soil Requirements Wheat grows best in loamy, well-drained clayey soils. Primary Tillage Primary tillage for wheat involves 4–6 ploughings and 2–3 plankings. The extent of tillage depends on the nature of the previous crop, the soil type, the soil condition, and the season. Seed Bed Preparation Seed bed preparation for wheat involves 2 cultivations and 2 plankings. Sowing Time For rainfed wheat, early sowing takes place from 15 to 31 October, mid sowing from November to December, and late sowing up to 15 January. For irrigated wheat, November is the optimum sowing time, with December as an alternative. Dab Method The Dab method is used for weed control. It involves achieving optimum moisture through a presoaking irrigation, followed by ploughing and planking, which buries the weeds in the soil and helps maintain soil fertility. Seed Rate The recommended seed rate for wheat is 100–120 kg per hectare. The exact rate depends on the sowing method, seed purity, germination percentage, and the variety being sown.
