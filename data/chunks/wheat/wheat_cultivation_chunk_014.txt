Armyworms and cutworms - caterpillar (larval) stages of certain moths that feed on leaves and can cause significant defoliation, particularly damaging during the early seedling and vegetative stages; cutworms in particular can cut young seedlings off at the base. 4. Wheat stem sawfly and other stem-boring insects - larvae bore into and feed within the stem, weakening it structurally and causing lodging or stem breakage, particularly near harvest. 5. Wheat aphid-transmitted and other viral diseases indirectly linked to insect pest pressure, underscoring the importance of aphid control not just for direct feeding damage but for disease prevention. 6. Rodents (rats and other small mammals) - can cause significant losses both to the standing crop (by cutting stems and consuming grain) and to harvested/stored grain if storage structures are not rodent-proof. 7. Storage pests (post-harvest) - various beetles and moths (such as grain weevils and flour beetles) can infest stored wheat grain if it is not dried to a safe moisture level and stored in clean, well-sealed, pest-proof structures. Management principles: regular field monitoring/scouting to detect pest buildup before it reaches damaging levels; timely sowing within the recommended window to avoid the crop's most vulnerable growth stages coinciding with peak pest activity periods; use of resistant or tolerant varieties where available; maintaining field hygiene by removing crop residues and alternate host weeds that can harbor pests between seasons; encouraging natural predators and beneficial insects where possible as part of integrated pest management; and applying recommended insecticides only when pest populations exceed locally established economic threshold levels, using the correct product, dose, and timing as advised by agricultural extension services, to avoid unnecessary cost, resistance development, and harm to beneficial insects.
