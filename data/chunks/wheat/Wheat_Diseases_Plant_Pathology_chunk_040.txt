The seeds can be freed from galls by soaking in strong solution of common salt at the rate of 40 lbs per twenty-five gallons of water. By doing so the galls float on the surface of solution. Crop rotation: crop rotation with barley and oats for 2-3 years is quite effective. During rotations, seasonal wetting of galls ensures release of larger which die in the absence of host (wheat). Resistant varieties: same varieties are resistant against this disease. They are: Sonora 63, Lerma Roja, NP 908 and S 227. Ear Cockle Disease of Wheat (A Nematode Disease) The pathogen is Anguina tritici. The base of wheat seedling or stem gets enlarged. Leaves of the affected seedling get twisted and crinkled. The affected ears are shorter and broader with very short or no awns on the glumes. The affected ears are greener than the healthy ones. Description of the Disease The disease is caused by the nematode, Anguina tritici. In a diseased head, grains are replaced by nematode galls (cockles). Each gall may contain 10,000 to 30,000 or more larvae. Adult females are about 3-4 mm long and 120 µ in diameter. They lay eggs and produce all the juvenile stages and adults in seed galls. The larvae can survive up to 28 years under dry conditions. The juveniles swim upward on the leaves through film of water and feed ecto-parasitically on leaves. As the inflorescences forms the juveniles enter the floral parts and produce third and fourth stage juveniles. Use only gall-free seed from a healthy crop.
