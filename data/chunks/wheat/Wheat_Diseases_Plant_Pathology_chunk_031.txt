The bunt-balls (spore-mass) remain covered by the pericarp in the beginning, but later on they burst and the spore-mass is exposed. The spore-mass smells strongly of rotten fish like other two bunts. The Karnal bunt of wheat is caused by Tilletia barclayana Sacc. and Sydow., synonym Neovossia indica (Mitra) Mundk. The spores are spherical, smooth-walled and measuring 22-49 microns in diameter. Prior to germination, the spores require a long resting period. On germination, the spore produces a short, stout basidium. The sporidia are produced in large numbers (60-120) at the apex of the basidium. The primary sporidia are needle-shaped and flexible. The secondary sporidia, if produced, are sickle-shaped. The disease is soil-borne as well as air-borne. The spores found in the soil germinate, forming a large number of needle-shaped sporidia (basidiospores), and sickle-shaped secondary sporidia. The sporidia are being carried to the inflorescence through the air currents, where they infect the ovaries and the developing kernels. Infection takes place only when the flowering occurs. Crop rotation: this method of control may be helpful to some extent. Resistant varieties: for effective and economical control, the resistant varieties should be grown. Some important resistant varieties are: Kalyan Sona, S 227, PV 18, HD 2012, 4513, 4519, IWP 72, 87, 127, 129, WL 885, 1581, HB 383, UP 2002, etc. Powdery Mildew of Wheat The pathogen is Erysiphe graminis DC. var tritici, class Ascomycetes, order Erysiphales, family Erysiphaceae. The powdery mildew of wheat is commonly found in those regions where there is enough of moisture during sowing time. This disease has no or little importance in the plains of India.
