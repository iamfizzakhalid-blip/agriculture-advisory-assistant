For combine harvesting, grain moisture content is typically allowed to drop to around 20-25% or lower before harvest to allow efficient threshing and reduce grain damage; for manual harvesting with sickles followed by stacking, drying, and threshing, timing is somewhat more flexible but still aims to avoid over-ripening. Harvesting too early results in shriveled, immature, lower-weight grain and increased moisture content that complicates storage; harvesting too late increases the risk of grain shattering and losses in the field due to wind, rain, lodging, or bird damage, as well as potential quality deterioration from weathering. Threshing separates grain from straw and chaff, either mechanically via combine harvester or threshing machine, or traditionally by treading/beating followed by winnowing. POST-HARVEST HANDLING AND STORAGE After harvesting and threshing, grain should be properly cleaned to remove chaff, weed seeds, and foreign material, and then dried to a safe moisture level, generally around 12% or below, before storage, since higher moisture content promotes mold growth, grain heating, and rapid insect pest buildup during storage. Grain should be stored in clean, dry, well-ventilated, and preferably pest-proof and rodent-proof storage structures, kept away from direct contact with damp floors or walls. Bags or bulk storage containers should be inspected periodically for signs of moisture ingress, insect infestation, or rodent activity, and grain should be aerated or turned periodically in bulk storage systems if facilities allow, to prevent moisture and temperature buildup. Proper post-harvest handling directly affects both the quantity of grain preserved and its quality and market value at the time of sale.
