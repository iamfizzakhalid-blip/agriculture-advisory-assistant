Like other rusts of wheat, the inoculum (uredospores) survives on the tillers and self sown plants in the hills. The inoculum survives at 5,000 feet and above. Every year the uredospores are carried by wind from the hills to the plains, where they cause infection. Loss Due to Rusts Rust pustules cover a greater part of the photosynthetic area and thus hinder the plant in its food manufacturing process. When pustules burst through the epidermis, the water is lost by transpiration resulting in partial sterility, poorly filled ears and shrivelled grains. If rust becomes severe before the milk stage of the crop, the losses are always great even resulting in crop failures. Physiologic Races of Wheat Rusts Stakman (1926) and his co-workers have defined physiologic specialization as the presence of entities within morphologic species, not readily distinguishable by structure, but differing from each other physiologically. These entities have been designated variously such as physiologic races, biologic species, biologic forms, specialized races, racial strains, physiologic forms, etc. Each physiologic race is denoted by a specific number such as Puccinia graminis tritici 15. Eriksson (1894) termed these races as 'formae speciales'.
