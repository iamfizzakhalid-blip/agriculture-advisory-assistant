GRAIN QUALITY PARAMETERS Wheat grain quality is assessed through several parameters relevant to milling, baking, and market value, including test weight (bulk density, an indicator of grain soundness and milling yield), grain protein content (important for bread-making strength; higher protein is generally desired for bread wheat while lower protein may be preferred for some pastry or biscuit applications), gluten content and strength (determines dough elasticity and bread-making performance), moisture content (affects both storability and, in many markets, the price received), and freedom from foreign material, shriveled or damaged grains, and disease-affected grain such as Karnal bunt-infected kernels. Grain quality is influenced by variety, growing conditions (particularly temperature and moisture stress during grain filling), nitrogen nutrition (higher nitrogen availability generally increases grain protein content, within limits), and post-harvest handling and storage conditions. ECONOMIC AND NUTRITIONAL IMPORTANCE Wheat is a major source of dietary energy, protein, and B-vitamins for a large portion of the world's population, particularly where it forms the base of staple foods such as bread and flatbreads. Economically, wheat is a critical crop for both smallholder food security and large-scale commercial agriculture, and it is one of the most widely traded agricultural commodities globally. Wheat straw, a by-product of grain production, has additional value as livestock fodder, bedding material, and in some regions as a raw material for other industrial uses, adding to the overall economic value of the crop beyond grain alone.
