All the grains of an ear are being converted into spore balls. The inside of the grain is filled with a greasy spore-mass smelling strongly of rotten fish. The spore-masses or bunt balls leave the grain contents while leaving the grain coats intact. The spores are liberated from the bunted grains with difficulty. The spore mass ruptures during threshing and the spores adhere to the surface of the healthy grains. Smooth-spored bunt of wheat is caused by Tilletia foetida (Wallr.) Liro. The spores possess the smooth wall and measure 6-25 microns in diameter. Spores germinate readily in water or moist air. On germination, the spore produces a promycelium (basidium). At the tip of promycelium, a cluster of 8-15 long, narrow, curved sporidia (basidiospores) is formed. The sporidia usually become united in pairs by lateral branches forming H-shaped structures. On germination, these structures give rise to secondary sickle-shaped sporidia. The infection can take place only when the germ tube of a primary or secondary sporidium comes in contact with a young seedling. The disease is externally seed-borne, and therefore systemic. The infection takes place through seed-borne spores. After infection, the fungal hyphae grow along the tip of the shoot, and at the time of development of the ear, concentrate in the inflorescence and the grains are being converted into smutted tissues, enclosing masses of bunt spores. The spore-mass can survive long under dry conditions. Resistant varieties: for effective and economical control, the resistant varieties should be grown.
