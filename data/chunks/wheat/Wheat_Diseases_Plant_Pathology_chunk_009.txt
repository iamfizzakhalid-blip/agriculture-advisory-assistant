The infection focus in Nepal is important for Uttar Pradesh as it is the source of disease in the crops of the plains along the Nepal border. In 1974, L. M. Joshi and his associates reported that black rust develops at an early date in South India which then serves as inoculum reservoir in the form of uredospores for the rest of the country. Brown rust of wheat has two foci of infection, the Himalayas in the North, and Nilgiri and Palini hills in the South. It is the most widely prevalent rust. The uredospores are transported by upper winds from Nilgiris and Palini hills, and then are washed down over Central India (Madhya Pradesh) by rains. The northern infection moves slowly southwards until the two populations merge. The air-borne inoculum of brown rust as of well as black rust and its deposition into southern hills (Nilgiri and Palini hills) has been traced by wind trajectories and weather satellite cloud images. From investigations of Nagarajan and Singh (1975), it appears that the tropical cyclones have a great bearing on the deposition of spores in Central India (Madhya Pradesh) within a time span of about 70 hours. The cyclonic disturbances occurring in the Bay of Bengal/Arabian Sea in late October or early November set a pattern of favourable winds which carry the inoculum (uredospores) of black and brown rusts from Southern foci to the target area in Madhya Pradesh along with rain clouds. It has been found that the invisible spore cloud movement can be successfully tagged with the visible cloud movements seen in satellite television cloud photographs.
