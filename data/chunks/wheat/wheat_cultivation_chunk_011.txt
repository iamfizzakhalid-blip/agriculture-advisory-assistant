MAJOR WHEAT DISEASES 1. Rusts (collectively the most economically damaging group of wheat diseases worldwide): - Stem (black) rust - caused by the fungus Puccinia graminis f. sp. tritici. Typically appears later in the season, producing elongated, reddish-brown to black, powdery pustules on stems, leaf sheaths, and leaves; can cause severe yield loss, including complete crop failure in susceptible varieties under favorable (warm, humid) conditions. - Leaf (brown/orange) rust - caused by Puccinia triticina (formerly Puccinia recondita f. sp. tritici). Forms small, circular to oval, orange-brown pustules mainly scattered on the upper leaf surface; the most widespread of the wheat rusts in many regions, causing chronic yield losses even where it does not reach epidemic levels. - Stripe (yellow) rust - caused by Puccinia striiformis f. sp. tritici. Appears as bright yellow pustules arranged in narrow stripes running along the leaf veins; favored by cool, humid weather and can spread very rapidly across large areas, causing major regional epidemics and yield losses when susceptible varieties dominate. 2. Smuts and bunts: - Flag smut - caused by Urocystis tritici (previously Urocystis agropyri). A soil- and seed-borne disease affecting leaves and stems, producing long black dusty streaks/sori that rupture to release spores; can kill the plant or severely stunt it, particularly damaging in regions with a history of repeated wheat monoculture. - Loose smut - caused by Ustilago tritici.
