In July 2025, the government imposed a further ban on exports of wheat flour, wheat products, refined flour, and semolina, which remained in place as of January 2026. Price History Domestic prices of wheat flour, Pakistan's main staple food, have shown a pattern of sharp fluctuations tied to harvest outcomes, import and export policy, currency depreciation, and production costs. Notable price episodes include a sharp increase between 2019 and early 2020 to near-record levels, driven by tight supply following below-average harvests in 2018 and 2019; a further surge to record or near-record levels by August 2022, up about 30 percent year-on-year, linked to low stocks, high input costs, and currency depreciation; continued near-record levels through October 2023, up about 85 percent year-on-year, and January 2024, up about 65 percent year-on-year, driven by tight market supply, high production and transport costs, and a sharp increase in the minimum support price in early 2023; a sharp decline between March and June 2024 due to abundant market supply from the record 2024 harvest and the decision to stop government procurement at a minimum support price, with prices 30 to 40 percent lower year-on-year by February 2025 and continuing at low levels through July 2025, about 50 percent below year-earlier levels; and a renewed surge between July 2025 and January 2026 of about 50 to 90 percent in most markets, reaching near-record levels again, driven by seasonal pressure, stock losses, and market disruptions from floods and landslides in August and September 2025, combined with the year-on-year decline in 2025 production and strong domestic demand.
