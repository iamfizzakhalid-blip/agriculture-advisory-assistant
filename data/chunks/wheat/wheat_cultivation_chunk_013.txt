Root and crown diseases: Various soil-borne fungi (such as Fusarium and Bipolaris species) can cause seedling blight, root rot, and crown rot, particularly under moisture stress, poor drainage, or continuous wheat cropping without rotation. Management principles across wheat diseases: use of resistant or tolerant varieties as the first line of defense (since resistance can break down over time as new pathogen races emerge, periodic variety updates are important); seed treatment with an appropriate fungicide before sowing to control seed-borne diseases such as loose smut and Karnal bunt; avoiding excessive nitrogen application, which promotes dense, humid canopies favorable to fungal disease development; ensuring proper field drainage to reduce root and crown disease risk; practicing crop rotation to reduce the buildup of soil-borne pathogens and residue-borne diseases; regular field monitoring/scouting, especially during cool, humid weather that favors rust and powdery mildew development; and timely application of foliar fungicides when disease is detected early and conditions favor its spread, following locally recommended products, rates, and timing. MAJOR WHEAT PESTS 1. Aphids (several species) - small, soft-bodied, sap-sucking insects that cluster on leaves, stems, and developing ears; they weaken the plant by removing plant sap, excrete honeydew that promotes sooty mold growth, reduce grain quality and yield when populations are high, and importantly can transmit viral diseases such as barley yellow dwarf virus. 2. Termites - soil-dwelling insects that can damage the root system and lower stem, especially in light, sandy soils and in drought-stressed crops; damaged plants dry out, show poor growth, and may lodge or die in patches, particularly visible as irregular yellowing/drying patches in the field. 3.
