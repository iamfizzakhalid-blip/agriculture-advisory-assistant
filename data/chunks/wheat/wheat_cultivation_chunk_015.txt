LODGING: CAUSES AND MANAGEMENT Lodging (the crop falling over or bending at the stem base) is a significant cause of yield loss and harvesting difficulty in wheat. Common causes include excessive nitrogen fertilizer application without a corresponding balance of potassium, which promotes tall, weak, and thin-walled stems; dense plant populations from an excessively high seed rate, which increases competition for light and produces thinner, weaker stems; use of tall, lodging-susceptible varieties, particularly under high-input, high-fertility conditions; strong winds or heavy rain, especially when combined with a saturated, waterlogged root zone that provides poor anchorage; and stem-boring insect damage or stem-based fungal disease that weakens the stem structurally. Management strategies include choosing shorter-strawed, lodging-resistant varieties where high inputs are used, applying nitrogen in appropriately timed and sized split doses rather than large single applications, ensuring adequate potassium supply, avoiding excessively high seed rates, and, in some systems, the use of plant growth regulators to reduce stem elongation and lodging risk under very high-fertility conditions. CROP ROTATION AND CROPPING SYSTEMS Wheat is commonly grown in rotation with a range of other crops depending on the region and irrigation availability, such as rice, cotton, maize, sugarcane, or various legume/pulse crops. Crop rotation offers multiple benefits: it helps break the life cycles of wheat-specific diseases and weeds (such as Phalaris minor and certain soil-borne pathogens) that build up under continuous wheat monoculture; rotating with legume crops can improve soil nitrogen status through biological nitrogen fixation, reducing fertilizer requirements for the following wheat crop; and diversifying crops spreads production
