Use only gall-free seed from a healthy crop. Remove the galls from seeds by winnowing or using a sieve of proper size. Galls can also be removed by soaking the seed in 2 per cent to 5 per cent common salt. When salt water is used, make sure that the treated seed is washed by plain water 2 to 3 times and then dried before sowing. Molya Disease of Wheat and Barley (A Nematode Disease) The pathogen causing molya disease is Heterodera avenae, the cereal cyst nematode, or cereal root eelworm, known to occur in most wheat, barley, and oat growing areas of the world. In India, the disease was first reported in 1957 from Punjab, and thereafter has been recorded from Rajasthan, Haryana and Western Uttar Pradesh. Occurrence of small patches of stunted, pale and greenish-yellow plants indicate the presence of the disease. The infected plants remain stunted in growth and do not grow beyond 30 cm to 40 cm from the ground level. Ears, if formed, have few or no grains. Main roots of the plants get elongated and contain few rootlets at the end. As many as 15 to 20 white or dark brown cysts may be present on a single plant root. Crop rotation with a non-cereal crop can prevent the disease. Since the disease is more prevalent in light soils with poor water holding capacity, it is suggested that the maintenance of good soil structure or fertility will be helpful in reducing damage by this disease.
