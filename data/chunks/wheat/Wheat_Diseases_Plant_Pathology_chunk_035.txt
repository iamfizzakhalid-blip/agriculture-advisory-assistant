Each end of the germ tube forms an appressorium which firmly attached to the surface and gives out a peg-like structure which penetrates the host plant. Once inside, the tube swells up and gives rise to a large, branched haustorium in the cavity of the cell. Two physiologic races of the fungus causing powdery mildew of wheat have been reported from India and four from U. S. A. The recurrence of the disease takes place through the cleistothecia (the perennating bodies). The cleistothecia perennate on the straw and plant debris after harvest, and provide the necessary inoculum (ascospores) for the next season. The primary infection is brought by ascospores, and the secondary infection is from the air-borne conidia. Resistant varieties: the best method of control is to sow the resistant varieties. In countries, like U. S. A. and Canada, much work has been done to evolve the resistant varieties. In India too, there is some progress in this direction. Varieties NP 710, NP 718, HW 741, C 591, C 750 are moderately resistant; Sharbati Sonara, Sonora 64, Chhoti Lerma are recommended for cool climatic regions (Uttarakhand and H. P.) of Northern India; HD-1980 and HD 2204 are also resistant. Recently developed varieties, such as HB 208, CPAN 1676, VL 401 and HD 2074 are found resistant against all races of E. graminis tritici. Chemical control: sulphur dusting has been proved to be fruitful in the control of this disease. But the cost of treatment is prohibitive and the control is not practical. Fungicidal sprays proved effective in control of this disease: spray with systemic fungicide, benlate (0.1%) gave good results. Spray of calixin is equally effective.
