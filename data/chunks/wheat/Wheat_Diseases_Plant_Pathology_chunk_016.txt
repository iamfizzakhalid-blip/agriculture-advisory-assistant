Its uredosori (uredospores/uredosorus) are small, oval, usually run together, burst late and with little fringes of the epidermis. Their colour is almost lemon-yellow, always arranged in long stripes, found on all green parts of the plant. Its telia or teleutosori (teleutopustules) are like uredosori but flattened dull black, and often run together. They do not burst through the epidermis (subepidermal). Arranged in rows, chiefly on under surface of leaves but also on all the green parts of the plant. Brown rust (P. recondita) attacks the leaf lamina almost exclusively, rarely the leaf sheaths and very rarely the stems. Its uredosori are small but larger than in yellow rust, oval or round, do not usually run together, burst early with a fringe of broken epidermis around them, always scattered on the surfaces of leaf lamina. Colour bright orange when fresh, found chiefly on the upper surface of leaves. Its teliosori (teleutopustules) often absent, when present resemble uredosori (uredopustules) but more flattened and dull. Do not burst through the epidermis (sub-epidermal), found chiefly on under surface of leaves; very rare elsewhere. Control Measures (Disease Management) for Wheat Rusts On the basis of the study of the life cycle of the rusts (black, brown, and yellow) and their annual recurrence in the Indian plains, a few important methods have been suggested. They are: clean-up campaign; cultural practices; chemical treatment; and resistant varieties. Clean-up campaign: in India, K. C. Mehta, as a result of his extensive study of wheat rusts (black, brown and yellow), recommended the vigorous destruction of self-sown plants and tillers which carry over the rust in uredo-stage and which are responsible for infection of the crop in the plains.
