Infected plants have sparse root development and characteristically a brown-black rot can be seen at the crown and extending to the base. Poor soil aeration, soil saturation and high penetration resistance to root development due to soil structural degradation can increase root rot and soil-borne pathogens. They can also reduce the ability of the root system to overcome the harmful effects of pathogens resident in the topsoil. The conservation of soil moisture, amelioration of soil compaction, the build up of organic matter and the promotion of good soil life (in terms of microbial biomass, diversity and activity) are factors that contribute to the development of healthy plants and the suppression of soil-borne diseases. They also help enable the plant to better resist the pressure of disease and insect attack.
