Flag Smut of Wheat The pathogen is Urocystis tritici Koerne., class Basidiomycetes, order Ustilaginales, family Ustilaginaceae. This disease is confined to the Punjab in India, while outside India it is known in Japan, Australia, China, and in some parts of Europe and U. S. A. The disease seems of much more restricted distribution than the other smuts of wheat. As the name indicates, the disease is usually found on the leaf blades. It is also common on the sheaths and occurs sometimes on the stem and very rarely on the ears. Every shoot of the plant may become infected; their leaves become twisted up, droop and ultimately withered. The complete plant dies up. The infection is more towards the upper portions of the leaf blade than at the base. Very frequently, the ears are replaced by a twisted mass of leaves. The ears do not bear any grains. Whenever the grain is formed, it is always much shrivelled and useless. On the infected leaf blade long, grey, slightly swollen bands appear parallel to the veins of the leaf. Ultimately, these elongated sori rupture and expose a black, powdery mass of spores. The disease is caused by Urocystis tritici Koerne. The spores are formed in tiny balls consisting of 1 to 4 fertile brown spores. These spores remain surrounded by a layer of light-coloured sterile cells. The spore balls are usually of oval, 9-16 microns in diameter. The sterile cells are small and elliptic. The spores germinate by producing a promycelium (basidium) or 1-3-celled germ-tube, which bears a cluster of 2 to 6 sporidia at the apex.
