Rice is also classified botanically into two major subspecies, indica and japonica, with indica types being the long, slender grained varieties traditionally grown across most of tropical and subtropical Asia, and japonica types being the shorter, roundish grained varieties more associated with temperate regions like Japan, Korea, and parts of China, though both are now grown in many places worldwide. In terms of farming system, rice is grown as lowland or paddy rice under flooded conditions, as upland or aerobic rice under non-flooded, more rainfed conditions, as deepwater rice in flood prone areas where the plant must be able to elongate rapidly to keep its top above rising floodwater, and as rainfed lowland rice, which sits somewhere between fully irrigated paddy systems and purely rainfed upland systems. As with maize, farmers also choose between open pollinated or traditional varieties, which breed true and allow seed saving, modern high yielding varieties developed through conventional breeding, and hybrid rice, which like hybrid maize is created by crossing specific parent lines to achieve higher yield through hybrid vigor but does not breed true, meaning seed saved from a hybrid crop will not perform as well if replanted, so farmers growing hybrid rice generally need to buy fresh seed each season, similar to the situation with hybrid maize.
