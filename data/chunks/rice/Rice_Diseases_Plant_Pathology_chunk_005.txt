Hot water treatment for seeds has been suggested, and in Taiwan, seed disinfection by a five-minute dip in a 0.1 percent solution of uspulum followed by covering the seeds with mats moistened with the same solution for six hours gave effective control. Spraying of Bordeaux mixture has proved quite effective against neck and node infection. Padmanabhan et al. (1956) recorded effective disease control through four Bordeaux mixture sprayings, two before and two after flowering of the crop. Spraying of other copper fungicides, such as perenox, coppesan, and cupravit, has also proved effective against blast disease of rice; dusting of organomercurials has also been suggested for control. Sanitation measures include collecting and destroying plant debris. Collateral hosts such as Panicum repens, Digitaria marginata, and Setaria intermedia should also be collected from paddy fields and destroyed, since plant debris and collateral hosts are thought to carry the fungus in the form of mycelium and conidia. Proper manuring means avoiding excess nitrogenous fertilizers, which predispose the plant to infection; manuring should be balanced with potassic and phosphatic manures. Brown Spot Disease of Rice The pathogen is Helminthosporium oryzae Breda de Haan, synonym Bipolaris oryzae (Breda de Haan) Shoemaker, belonging to class Deuteromycetes, order Moniliales, family Dematiaceae. The perfect stage of the fungus is Cochliobolus miyabeanus (Ito and Kuribayashi) Dreschler ex Dastur, of Ascomycetes. Brown spot disease, also called Sesame leaf spot or Helminthosporiose, is an important disease of rice reported from all rice-growing countries of the world. In India, this disease was known as one of the principal causes of the famous Bengal famine of 1942-43.
