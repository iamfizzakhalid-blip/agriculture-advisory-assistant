Gradually an extensive cavity is formed between the seed covering and the endosperm, and the spore-mass may entirely replace the starch cells of the endosperm, or may form only a layer on the endosperm. It has been reported that high and frequent rains, when the plants flower, favour disease incidence. In deep water, the spores have less chance of germination and causing infection. Excess of nitrogenous fertilizers also favours disease incidence (Lu and Li, 1955). Since only a few grains in an ear are infected, this disease has attracted little attention. It has been recommended that the seed material should be steeped in water and all the light and floating grains should be removed. In India, several resistant varieties have also been suggested; they include T 6, T 5, T 27, NCH 20, NMCH 43, Russ 1331, 2877, Hung 2115, HR 22, BJ 1, T 38, T 36, T 26, T 23, T 10, T 22, T 108, N 28, T 19, NCH 7, N 12, among others. False Smut or Green Smut of Rice The pathogen is Ustilaginoidea virens (Cke.) Tak., class Deuteromycetes, order Moniliales, family Dematiaceae. The disease was first recorded from South India in 1878. Since then it has been reported from all rice-growing countries of the world except Egypt and Italy. The disease has been reported from all the rice-growing states of India, causing much loss in Andhra Pradesh. This disease infects the spikelets alone and may be seen only in the ears, where it develops its fructification in the ovary of individual grains.
