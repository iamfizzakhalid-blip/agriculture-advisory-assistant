It was first reported by Sundararaman from Tamil Nadu in 1919 and has since been prevalent in all rice-growing states of India, causing serious losses in West Bengal, Assam, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Orissa, and Uttar Pradesh. All parts of the plant are infected except the roots. Sometimes necrotic lesions are present even on the first emerging leaves; in severe cases, seedlings become blighted and die. The most characteristic symptom is the development of dark brown spots on the upper surface of the leaf lamina, generally beginning after transplanting. The spots develop on both the leaf lamina and leaf sheath, are isolated, dark brown, ellipsoidal or oval, and scattered throughout the lamina and sheath. A fully developed spot has a greyish-brown central region surrounded by a deep purplish-brown margin, while smaller spots are uniformly purplish-brown in colour. In early stages of development, a yellowish halo usually surrounds the spots, which vary in size, ranging from 1 to 14 mm in length and 0.5 to 5 mm in width. In severe cases, the spots coalesce and the leaves become dried and wilted; spots also develop on the leaf sheath and the culm below the ear, and the base of the panicle becomes shrunk and discoloured. The inflorescence and individual spikelets also become infected. If the inflorescence is attacked at an early stage, it fails to develop. Grains formed inside affected spikelets become discoloured and shrivelled, and sometimes the fungus infects healthy grains in the interior region; there is always a low percentage of germination when infected grains are sown, and post-emergence seedling blight is often observed.
