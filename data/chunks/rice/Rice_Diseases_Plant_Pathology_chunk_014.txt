Ultimately the nodes become rotten, and the culm breaks at this point when pulled. The most peculiar symptom of this disease is the elongation of plants, which is followed by the death of the plants. The fungus causing the disease is known as Fusarium moniliforme Sheld., with Gibberella fujikuroi (Saw.) Wollenweber, an ascomycetous fungus, as the perfect stage. The fungus may grow readily on a number of media, producing luxuriant mycelium. The hyphae are slender, 3 to 5 microns broad, closely septate and much branched. Microconidia are formed in chains; each microconidium is 1 to 2-celled, elliptic to ovate or oval in shape, and measures 5 to 12 by 2 to 4 microns. The macroconidia are falcate, narrow at both ends, 2 to 5-celled, and measure 30 to 50 by 3.0 microns. Chlamydospores are produced rarely. In the perfect stage (Gibberella fujikuroi), the perithecia have been observed in Japan on the culms of dead plants; however, the perithecia have not been recorded from India. They are superficial, globose, dark brown, and measure 270 to 350 by 240 to 300 microns. The clavate asci are formed in the perithecia, measuring 75 to 130 by 9 to 16 microns; each ascus contains 4 or 6 ascospores, which are long, ellipsoid, one-septate, and measure 10 to 24 by 4 to 9 microns. Active substances, such as gibberellins, have been isolated from the fungus. The gibberellin was found mixed with fusaric acid.
