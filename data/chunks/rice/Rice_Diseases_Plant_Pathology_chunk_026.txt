Striga on Rice The parasite is Striga lutea Lour., division Phanerogams, class Dicotyledons, sub-class Gamopetalae, order Personales, family Scrophulariaceae. Striga lutea is prevalent on rice in Kerala. Severe damage is caused in Kerala by Striga only in rice cultivated on 'modan' lands, under rain-fed conditions. In Kerala, the parasites begin to appear from the middle of June; they dry up before heading. Patches of varying dimensions with few or no surviving plants may be seen in the fields. In later infestation, the affected plants become stunted with poorly developed ears; the leaves dry up, and the infected patches present a blighted appearance. Sometimes the yield is sufficiently reduced. Numerous shoots of the parasite are seen at the base of affected plants. Parasite Striga lutea is a herbaceous plant. It remains attached to the roots of the host plant by means of suckers or haustoria. Above the soil, one or more shoots appear, which remain attached to a well-developed underground stem. Generally, it extends beneath the soil surface about two inches in depth. The aerial shoots are 6 to 15 inches in height. The stem is quadrangular and bears sessile opposite narrow leaves. Whitish yellow flowers develop in the axils of the leaves in later stages, and the capsules are developed, which contain numerous minute seeds.
