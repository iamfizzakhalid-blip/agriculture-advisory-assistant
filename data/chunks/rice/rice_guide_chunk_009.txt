However, water is often deliberately drained for short periods at certain points, such as briefly around active tillering to encourage stronger root growth, and again before harvest to allow the field to dry out enough for easy harvesting, whether by hand or machine. As with maize, the flowering period is the single most water sensitive stage, and allowing a flooded rice field to dry out and stress the crop right around flowering can cause serious losses in grain set, so this is generally the point at which water management is most carefully protected. In many regions, a water saving technique called alternate wetting and drying has become popular, in which the field is allowed to dry down to a certain point before being reflooded, rather than being kept continuously flooded throughout the season, and this can significantly reduce total water use without a major yield penalty if managed carefully, though it requires more active monitoring than simply keeping the field flooded at all times. Weed management in rice is closely tied to water management, since maintaining standing water is itself one of the most effective ways to suppress weeds by starving them of the oxygen and light most weed seeds need to germinate and establish.
