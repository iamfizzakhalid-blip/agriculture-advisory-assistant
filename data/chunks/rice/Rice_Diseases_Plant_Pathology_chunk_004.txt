A high relative humidity of 92 percent and free water are required for conidial germination and infection. When night temperature is about 20°C, alternated with a day temperature of about 30°C, for about 14 hours light and 10 hours darkness, the crop favours disease incidence. The use of nitrogenous fertilizers increases crop susceptibility to the disease; it was recorded at Coimbatore that applying over 40 lbs of nitrogen per acre increased infection intensity in susceptible varieties. The age of the plant also influences infection intensity: the fungus can infect seedlings 25 days old but not those three months old, and upper younger leaves of seedlings are more susceptible than middle or lower older leaves. As the plant ages, resistance to infection increases until the ear emerges, when plants become susceptible again to neck and nodal infections. The most economic method of control is cultivating resistant, high-yielding varieties. Breeding for blast resistance has been carried out in India for the last 5 to 6 decades; some resistant cultivars developed for the disease include Co 4, TKM-1, Co 29, Co 30, T-603, T-141, A-67, A-90, A-200, A-249, and IR-579. Seed treatment methods include immersion of seeds in a 0.2 percent solution of Kalimat B for 24 hours, which controls the disease and promotes seedling growth. Seed protectants such as agrosan GN, cerasan, phygon, and spergon have proved effective. Seed treatment with organomercurial compounds (Padwick, 1950) is also effective when seeds are taken from a diseased crop.
