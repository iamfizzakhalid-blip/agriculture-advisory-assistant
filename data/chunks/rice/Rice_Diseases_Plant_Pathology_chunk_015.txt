Gibberellins are used as growth regulators or growth promoting substances. Collateral hosts also exist for this pathogen. Fusarium moniliforme var. majus, which causes 'top rot of sugarcane', can also cause 'foot rot of rice'. Generally, the disease is externally seed-borne; there is also evidence to show that the disease may sometimes be soil-borne. The infested stubbles and plant debris left in the field after harvest also cause infection through the soil. Soil temperature and soil moisture influence the intensity of the disease. The optimum temperature for the survival of the pathogen lies between 25°C and 30°C. Excess of nitrogenous manures increases the intensity of the disease. Since the disease is seed-borne, various methods of seed treatment have been suggested for its control. When grains are steeped in 1 percent formalin for 15 minutes, infection is sufficiently reduced. Seed treatment with several organomercury compounds such as agrosan GN, ceresan, harvesan at the rate of 2 grams per kg of seed has proved very effective. Seed dressing with ceresan or agrosan GN has been adopted quite successfully in South India. Successful control has also been achieved by hot-water treatment of the grains at 55°C for half an hour. The most economic method of control is the cultivation of resistant, high-yielding varieties. Bunt of Rice The pathogen is Tilletia horrida Tak., class Basidiomycetes, order Ustilaginales, family Tilletiaceae. The disease was first described by Takahashi from Japan in 1896, and has since been found widely distributed in South and East Asia including India, Myanmar, Thailand, Indo-china, Indonesia, Philippines, and China.
