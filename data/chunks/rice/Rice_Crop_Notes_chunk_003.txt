In Pakistan, Basmati rice undergoes a hot water (heat) treatment before shelling, which results in less breakage and easier cooking. Varieties — Grain Type Coarse varieties have rough leaves and are short-statured, while fine varieties have plain leaves and are long-statured. Nursery A nursery of 6–8 marlas is sufficient to transplant one acre. Transplanting is done after puddling. Nursery raising methods include the dry method, wet method, Rab method, and seed priming. A nursery that is 25–30 days old can be transplanted. Wet method: This is practiced in clayey and clay loam soils where standing water can be maintained. The selected field is filled with water 25–30 days before sowing the nursery. It is better to plow the field twice under dry or watery conditions to break soil clods and create better puddled conditions. After watering, the field is plowed twice, followed by planking; this is repeated after 7–10 days depending on water availability. Dry method: This is practiced under dry soil conditions. Seed beds of convenient dimensions are prepared by raising the soil to a height of about 5–10 cm. A thin layer of farmyard manure or half-burnt paddy husk may be spread on the nursery bed to facilitate uprooting. Soaked seed is spread over the seedbed and then irrigated. Rab method: The seed bed is covered with 3–5 inches of dry refuse, which is set on fire in April–May. This results in some loss of nitrogen but faster availability of other nutrients such as potassium, which is usually the limiting factor.
