Importance Rice is the second major cereal after wheat. It provides about 60% of the energy needs of half of the world's population. Rice occupies about 10% of the cropped area in Pakistan, including the Kallar tract. The grain contains about 80% carbohydrates and 7% protein. Pakistan ranks 4th in the world in rice exports. Climate Requirements Rice grows well in hot, humid tropical conditions, requiring about 1000 mm of annual rainfall and a temperature of around 20°C. Soil Requirements Rice grows best in soils with 40–60% clay content. It is not successful in sandy soils but is fairly resistant to salinity. Growth Stages of Rice Germination Seedling growth Tillering Stem elongation Booting Inflorescence Anthesis Milk development Dough development Ripening Root Development Rice roots are fibrous and shallow. They contain aerenchyma cells, which supply oxygen to the plant while it stands in water. Shoot Development The rice shoot has a jointed stem with hollow internodes and pithy nodes. Coarse varieties are short-statured, while fine varieties are long-statured. Inflorescence The rice inflorescence is a branched spike, with each branch ending in a grain. The grain has an outer covering called the husk or hull. When the husk is not removed, the grain is called paddy (unpolished); when the husk is removed, it is called rice (polished). Shelling Shelling refers to the removal of the husk. Paddy can germinate, but rice (after husk removal) cannot. In Pakistan, Basmati rice undergoes a hot water (heat) treatment before shelling, which results in less breakage and easier cooking.
