Physiologic Races Several physiologic races of Pyricularia oryzae have been recognized based on infection types on different hosts from artificial inoculation; about 28 races have been recognized so far. Nature of Disease Blast disease of rice is thought to be seed-borne in nature. Kuribayashi (1928) found that seedlings became infected when seeds bearing conidia on their surface were sown; in some cases, conidia were found present between the husk and the caryopsis. Seedlings raised from such infected seeds soon died. In India, when seeds from disease-prevalent areas were used for sowing, new outbreaks were reported in new localities, supporting seed-borne transmission. The disease is also carried over on collateral hosts such as Panicum repens and Digitaria marginata, which grow in rice fields and are heavily infected by P. oryzae, serving as sources of infection. The intensity of infection is greatly influenced by environmental factors, which play a significant role in epidemic development. Seedlings grown in water-covered seed beds show less infection than those grown in dry seed beds. Nurseries without adequate water supply show severe infection, while flooding increases plant resistance. Low soil temperature (15°C to 20°C) is largely responsible for the incidence of blast disease. Frequent rains, continuous cloudy weather, and high humidity at the critical tillering and heading period favour higher disease incidence. Spore germination is stimulated especially at temperatures of 24°C to 26°C, which is optimum for infection. A high relative humidity of 92 percent and free water are required for conidial germination and infection.
