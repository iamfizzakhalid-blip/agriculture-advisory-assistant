As with maize, birds and rodents can also cause meaningful losses, particularly as the crop nears maturity and the grain becomes attractive to them, and integrated pest management, relying on regular field monitoring, resistant varieties, conservation of natural enemies like spiders and parasitic wasps that are often abundant in flooded rice systems, and targeted rather than routine pesticide use, is generally the recommended approach. Diseases are also a major concern in rice. Rice blast, caused by a fungus, is one of the most economically damaging rice diseases worldwide and can affect leaves, stems, and the panicle itself, appearing as diamond shaped lesions with gray centers on leaves, and if it infects the neck of the panicle it can cause the entire panicle to die and produce little or no grain; management relies heavily on resistant varieties along with fungicide use in severe cases. Bacterial leaf blight causes water soaked streaks along the leaf margins that turn yellow and then gray as the leaf dies back from the tip, and it spreads readily in warm, humid, windy conditions. Sheath blight, another fungal disease, produces irregular greenish gray lesions on the leaf sheath near the waterline and can spread up the plant under dense, heavily fertilized crop canopies. Various viral diseases, such as tungro, are transmitted by insect vectors like leafhoppers and cause stunting and yellow or orange discoloration of the leaves, and since there is no direct cure once a plant is infected, management again focuses on controlling the insect vectors and using resistant varieties where available.
