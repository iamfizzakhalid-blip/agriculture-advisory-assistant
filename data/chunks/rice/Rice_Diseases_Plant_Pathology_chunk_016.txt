In India, the disease has been reported from various states such as Assam, Bihar, Orissa, Punjab, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, and Kerala. The disease is confined to the grains in the panicle, often one to nine grains on an ear in a few individual plants, with spore sori produced almost hidden by the enclosing unaffected glumes. In other cases, the glumes are forced a little apart and a black mass of spores is extruded. Sometimes minute black streaks are visible, bursting between lemma and palea. In certain cases the entire kernel is destroyed while in other cases the kernel is only partially affected. Partially infected kernels become twisted and project outside the husk; the spores are sticky and adhere to the surface of the glumes and even to the outside of the healthy grains in contact with affected ones. At maturity of the sori, leaves adjacent to the infected ears become dusted with the black spores. It has also been recorded that affected plants become stunted and produce fewer tillers than healthy ones. The bunt of rice is caused by Tilletia horrida Tak. Padwick and Khan (1944) revised the name of the pathogen to Neovossia horrida (Tak.) Pad. and Khan; however, Tullis and Johnson (1952) preferred the name N. barclayana. Once again the name of the pathogen has been revised as Tilletia horrida. Some graminaceous collateral hosts have also been reported. The mycelium may be seen in the stem of affected plants, running between cells of the vertical sub-epidermal row of parenchyma, under the stomata.
