Nitrogen deficiency in rice shows up as an overall pale, yellowish green color in older leaves and stunted growth, quite similar in principle to the deficiency symptoms seen in maize. Phosphorus is important for root development and early tillering, and is typically applied as a basal dose before or at transplanting, since like in maize it does not move readily through the soil; a phosphorus deficient rice plant often shows dark green, sometimes purplish, stunted leaves. Potassium supports stalk strength, disease resistance, and grain filling, much as it does in maize, and deficiency appears as yellowing or browning starting at the leaf tips and margins of older leaves. Zinc deficiency is also very common in flooded rice soils specifically because flooding tends to reduce the availability of zinc to the plant, and it shows up as stunted growth along with brownish, rusty colored spots or streaks on the leaves, a condition sometimes called khaira disease by farmers in parts of South Asia, and it is typically corrected with zinc sulfate application either to the soil or as a foliar spray. Water management for lowland rice, beyond the general need for a large total water supply, generally involves maintaining a shallow standing water layer, often somewhere around two to five centimeters deep, for most of the vegetative and reproductive period, since continuous shallow flooding tends to support both good yields and strong weed suppression.
