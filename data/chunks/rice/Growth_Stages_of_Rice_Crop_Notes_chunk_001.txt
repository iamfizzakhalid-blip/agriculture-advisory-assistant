Growth Stages of Rice Crop Source: Tara Group Pakistan Introduction Rice is a cereal belonging to the family Poaceae. As a tropical crop, it can be grown during the year's two distinct seasons, dry and wet, provided sufficient water is available. Rice is normally an annual crop, but in the tropics it can survive as a perennial, producing a ratoon crop. Overview of Growth Stages The growth stages of rice are germination, seedling, tillering, internode elongation, booting, heading, flowering, milking, dough, and maturation. Germination Seed germination occurs when the seed coat has absorbed adequate water to become soft and elastic. The ideal temperature for rice germination is 87°F, or 30°C. Seedling Seedling emergence occurs when the first internode, called the mesocotyl, has elongated and pushed the tip of the rice coleoptile, also called the epiblast, through the soil surface. The appearance of the coleoptile is a telltale sign of seedling emergence. Tillering Tillering usually begins at the fifth-leaf stage, referred to as V5, when the first tiller becomes visible and emerges from the axillary bud of the second leaf on the culm. Tillering continues synchronously with the nth leaf of the main culm, with each tiller emerging from the axillary bud of the (n-3)th leaf. The first tiller that emerges is called the primary tiller. Secondary tillers grow from the primary tillers, and tertiary tillers are produced from the secondary tillers. Internode Elongation Internode elongation begins about the time panicle initiation is occurring, continues until full plant height is reached, and is followed by heading. The process of panicle differentiation in rice happens while internode and panicle growth is ongoing.
