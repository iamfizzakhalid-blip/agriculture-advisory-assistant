FAO GIEWS — Pakistan Rice Compiled from the FAO/GIEWS Country Brief (current issue and archive), the Agricultural Stress Index dataset, and related FAO/GIEWS tools Current Situation (Reference Date: 6 February 2026) Paddy (rice) production in 2025 is estimated at 14 million tonnes, which is above the five-year average but 4 percent below the 2024/25 harvest. Despite an above-average area planted with rice, severe flooding caused crop losses, particularly in Punjab province. Rice is Pakistan's major exportable cereal, and exports of rice are forecast at 4.8 million tonnes for calendar year 2026. Planting of the 2026 paddy crop is affected by the same broad weather patterns described for wheat: dry weather between October and mid-December 2025 was followed by a return to near-average precipitation in late December and January, and remote sensing data indicated satisfactory vegetation conditions across most of the country as of late January 2026. Recent Production History Pakistan's paddy production has generally trended upward over the past decade, though it has been affected periodically by drought, floods, and shifts in planted area driven by relative crop prices. Selected officially estimated or forecast production figures reported over recent years include: 10.1 million tonnes in 2014; 9.9 million tonnes in 2015 (6 percent below the 2014 level, due to a contraction in area planted in response to large stocks and high production costs); around 10 to 10.3 million tonnes in 2016; 11.1 million tonnes in 2017 (a record level at the time, 8 percent above the prior good level, supported by area expansion linked to remunerative prices and continued input subsidies); 12.3 million tonnes in 2020 (a record level, reflecting a high level of plantings in response to remunerative prices, though with some flood and locust-related damage in Sindh province); a preliminary forecast of 13.2 million tonnes for 2022 that
