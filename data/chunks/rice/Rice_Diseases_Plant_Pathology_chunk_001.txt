Chand Publishing — Chapter 14, Diseases of Cereals Blast Disease of Rice The pathogen causing blast disease of rice is Pyricularia oryzae Cav., belonging to class Deuteromycetes, order Moniliales, family Moniliaceae. This disease has been reported from all the rice-growing countries of the world and is considered the most important disease affecting the crop. In India, it is more commonly found in the southern rice-growing areas, causing heavy losses in Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, and Orissa, and has also been reported from Maharashtra, Gujarat, Punjab, Kashmir, Bihar, West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, and Assam. The blast disease affects the crop at all stages, infecting all aerial parts of the plant, including the leaf blades, leaf sheaths, rachis, and joints of the culms, and sometimes even the glumes. Characteristic isolated, bluish-green, water-soaked necrotic lesions form on the leaf blades, broad in the centre with narrow elongations at the top and bottom. Under favourable atmospheric conditions, the lesions increase in number and ultimately coalesce to form large areas, leading to drying of the leaves; in some cases seedlings wither, die, or collapse when lesions develop on the culm. After transplantation, necrotic lesions appear on both the leaf lamina and leaf sheath, spindle-shaped and grey in the centre with brown and yellowish surrounding zones, with the outer rim of the lesion turning dark brown. In older lesions, the centre becomes grey or straw-coloured, and severely infected fields take on a blasted appearance. The pathogen also invades the nodal tissue of the culms, causing the tissue to shrink and turn dark grey or almost black; the culms may break at the infected nodes, with blackening extending 1 to 2 cm.
