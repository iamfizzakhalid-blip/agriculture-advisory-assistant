Booting The booting stage is loosely defined as the period characterized by a swelling of the flag leaf sheath, which is caused by an increase in the size of the panicle as it grows up the leaf sheath. Booting is the stage in which meiosis occurs. Heading Heading, referred to as the R3 growth stage, is the time when the panicle begins to exsert from the boot. Heading begins at about 60 days, depending on the variety grown. During this stage, the cells of the plant stem enlarge rapidly and vertically, pushing the panicle upwards. The stem becomes soft during this period and susceptible to attack by stem borer. Flowering Flowering refers to the events between the opening and closing of the spikelet, or floret, and lasts for 1 to 2½ hours, during which time pollination occurs. Flowering generally begins upon panicle exsertion or on the following day, and is consequently considered synonymous with heading. Ripening The grain filling and ripening, or maturation, phase covers the R6 to R8 growth stages. It follows fertilization of the ovary and is characterized by grain growth. During this period, the grain increases in size and weight. The grain changes color from green to gold or straw color at maturity, and the leaves of the rice plant begin to senesce. Milking At the milking stage, the developing starch grains in the kernel are soft, and the interior of the kernel is filled with a white liquid resembling milk. Dough The dough stage has two phases.
