The grains are being transformed into large, velvety, green masses which may become more than the diameter of the normal grain. Embedded in this mass and visible on the two sides are the lemma and palea. In initial stages of development, the mass is orange-yellow in colour, but soon turns dark green or almost black. The glumes are unaltered and are found closely applied to the centre of the green mass, which bursts out above and laterally from between them. Only a few grains in each panicle are usually affected, and they may occupy any part of the ear. The parasite was first described as a true smut under the name Ustilago virens by Cooke. On the basis of his studies, Takahashi renamed the fungus as Ustilaginoidea virens (Cke.) Tak. in 1951. The young ovary is infected by the parasite at an early stage of its development, and is transformed into a hard mass of closely-united, fine, colourless hyphae. This sclerotium-like body grows and bursts out literally between the closely-pressed glumes. Its outer layers develop spores of a brownish-green colour when ripe, but the centre of the mass consists of pseudoparenchyma which have entirely replaced the tissues of the grain. Towards the surface the hyphae are arranged radially, branched and septate, while the exterior consists of a mass of loosely adhering spores. The youngest spores are almost colourless and are found on the hyphae bounding the white centre of sclerotium. The mature spores are greenish-brown, and the outermost layer consists of these with a few fragments of the sporiferous hyphae.
