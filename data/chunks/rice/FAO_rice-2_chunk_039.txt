Inside the husk, the familiar white grain is covered by a layer called bran. The embryo, a small structure at the base of the grain, is also contained within the bran layer. Together, the grain, embryo and bran are called brown rice (Figure 1). Rough or paddy rice consists of an outer protective layer covering the husk (17-20 percent), the bran (10- 13 percent) and the white rice (70-71 percent). Paddy rice is the whole grain as taken off the plant at harvesting; the white rice grain which is eaten accounts for less than three-quarters of the weight of a paddy rice grain which also includes the hull and bran (Figure 2).
