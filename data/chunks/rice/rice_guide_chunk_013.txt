Threshing, meaning separating the grains from the panicle and straw, is done either as part of a combine harvester's operation or as a separate step afterward using a mechanical thresher or, in some smallholder systems, by beating harvested bundles against a hard surface. The harvested grain at this stage is called paddy or rough rice, still encased in its tough outer husk, and this husk is removed through a milling process to produce brown rice, and if the bran layer is further polished away, white rice; milling and storage conditions both significantly affect the final quality, breakage rate, and shelf life of the rice that eventually reaches consumers. Rice is used overwhelmingly as a direct human food staple, eaten as whole cooked grain in an enormous range of dishes across nearly every rice growing culture, and it is also processed into rice flour, rice noodles, rice paper, breakfast cereals, and fermented products like rice wine in various regional cuisines. The husk and bran removed during milling, while not eaten directly by most people, have their own uses, with rice bran sometimes pressed for oil and both husk and bran commonly used as animal feed ingredients or as fuel or organic matter in some systems. Rice straw, the leftover stems and leaves after harvest, is used in many regions as animal fodder, as material for thatching or crafts, or is incorporated back into the soil or, in some places, burned, though residue burning has become an increasing environmental and air quality concern in several major rice growing regions.
