What is the difference between stem (black) rust, leaf (brown) rust, and stripe (yellow) rust in terms of their causal pathogen, appearance on the plant, and the weather conditions that favor each?

Assistant:
- **Stem (black) rust**  
  - *Causal pathogen*: **Puccinia graminis f. sp. tritici**  
  - *Appearance*: elongated, reddish-brown to black powdery pustules on stems, leaf sheaths, and leaves.  
  - *Favourable weather*: warm, humid conditions; heavy dew or rain, moderately high temperatures, and strong wind aid spore spread.  

- **Leaf (brown/orange) rust**  
  - *Causal pathogen*: **Puccinia triticina** (formerly *Puccinia recondita* f. sp. tritici)  
  - *Appearance*: small, circular to oval orange-brown pustules mainly on the upper leaf surface.  
  - *Favourable weather*: not specified in the provided information.  

- **Stripe (yellow) rust**  
  - *Causal pathogen*: **Puccinia striiformis f. sp. tritici**  
  - *Appearance*: bright yellow pustules arranged in narrow stripes along leaf veins; can also appear on leaf blades, sheaths, stalks, glumes, pericarp, and kernels.  
  - *Favourable weather*: cool, humid conditions; heavy rains can cause severe damage; appears earlier, around mid-January.

what is maize

Assistant:
Maize (scientific name *Zea mays* L.) is an annual, short-day cereal crop in the Poaceae family. It is cross-pollinated, has a determinate growth habit, and its seed is a caryopsis. Maize is grown mainly for grain, but its kernels are also used for flour, oil, popcorn, and as fresh or dried fodder. In Pakistan it is locally called Makai (Urdu) or Maki (Punjabi).
